§2 · The agentic generation loop

The core conceptual shift: generation is no longer a single forward pass $p_\theta(\text{image}\mid\text{prompt})$ but a policy rollout in which image generation, code execution, search, and self-critique are all actions:

The Muse Image rollout: reasoning selects among tools, generation, and self-critique; the loop repeats until the model accepts its own output. Every arrow is a policy decision that RL can shape.

Why tools inside the sampler matter

  • Code as a renderer: text-in-image, charts, and QR codes are notoriously hard for pure diffusion/AR decoders because they require exact symbolic structure. Rendering them with code and conditioning the generator on the rendered figure sidesteps the hardest failure mode instead of scaling past it.
  • Search as grounding: real-time facts and reference images make the model robust to knowledge cutoff — the same retrieval-vs-parametric-knowledge trade-off as RAG (Day 4 §4), applied to pixels.
  • Model handoff: calling Muse Spark for hard reasoning is a compound-AI-system design: specialize components, route by difficulty (inference: likely a learned or heuristic router — good question to ask them).
◆ Frontier trick "Conditioning on rendered figures" means the tool output re-enters the context as an image. That requires an any-to-any backbone that treats interleaved text/image tokens uniformly — consistent with Muse Image "extending the Muse Spark architecture" (inference: a unified multimodal transformer with a visual tokenizer, not a bolted-on diffusion head; connects to your VLM_VLA_Unified_Omni notes).

§3 · RL & emergent self-refinement

Meta's most interesting claim: self-refinement was not designed — it emerged during RL. This is the image-generation analogue of "wait, let me reconsider" behaviors emerging in reasoning-model RL.

The objective (inference: standard frontier recipe)

Treat the whole rollout $\tau$ (reasoning + tool calls + generations + critiques) as one trajectory and optimize a KL-regularized objective:

$$\max_\theta \; \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta}\!\left[ r(\tau) \right] \;-\; \beta\, \mathrm{KL}\!\left(\pi_\theta \,\|\, \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\right)$$

With a group-relative estimator (your GRPO prep transfers directly): sample $G$ rollouts per prompt, and use

$$A_i = \frac{r_i - \mathrm{mean}(r_{1..G})}{\mathrm{std}(r_{1..G})}$$

Why refinement emerges: if a trajectory that inserts a critique-then-edit step earns higher reward than one that stops early, the advantage signal reinforces the whole habit — no explicit "self-correct" supervision needed. The credit-assignment question (which step in a long rollout earned the reward?) is exactly your GAE/advantage material.

The hard part: the reward is not verifiable

  • Image quality has no unit test. The reward is (inference) a blend of learned preference/aesthetic reward models, instruction-faithfulness scorers (e.g., VLM-judges checking object counts, spatial relations, text rendering), and verifiable sub-rewards where possible (does the QR code scan? does the plot match the data?).
  • Reward hacking risk: a preference RM rewards glossy, high-contrast images → the policy drifts toward "AI look" regardless of the instruction. Mitigations: RM ensembles, fresh human data, KL anchor, adversarial probing of the RM.
  • Notice the elegant loophole: tool-based sub-rewards are verifiable. Code output either matches or it doesn't. Pushing generation through tools converts an unverifiable-reward problem into a partially verifiable one (inference — but a strong hypothesis to offer in the interview).
⚙ In practice If asked "how would you RL-train an image model?", structure the answer: (1) define the action space (tokens? tool calls? full rollout?), (2) define reward (preference RM + faithfulness judges + verifiable tool checks), (3) pick the estimator (GRPO/RLOO for group baselines — cite your RL pack), (4) name the failure modes (reward hacking, mode collapse onto safe styles, critique loops that never terminate) and the guards (KL, max-turn budget, RM refresh).

§4 · Test-time compute scaling

Meta reports an ≈ log-linear relationship between inference compute and quality, and that deliberate (sequential) reasoning beats best-of-N. The intuition, made quantitative:

Best-of-N flattens

Model per-sample quality as i.i.d. $q_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ and pick the best of $N$. Extreme-value theory gives

$$\mathbb{E}\!\left[\max_{i \le N} q_i\right] \;\approx\; \mu + \sigma\sqrt{2 \ln N}$$

so doubling compute buys ever-shrinking gains ($\sqrt{\ln N}$), and — crucially — BoN can never exceed the ceiling of the base distribution's support. It only selects; it doesn't improve.

Sequential refinement compounds

Each refinement step conditions on the previous attempt and its critique, shifting the sampling distribution itself: $\mu_{t+1} > \mu_t$. If each round fixes a constant fraction of remaining defects, quality follows $Q(C) \approx a + b \ln C$ — genuinely log-linear, and unbounded by the base distribution.

Illustrative curves (not Meta's data): sequential refinement $a + b\ln C$ vs best-of-N $\mu + \sigma\sqrt{2\ln N}$, matched at $C{=}1$. The gap widens with compute — this is Meta's "deliberate reasoning outperforms best-of-N" claim.
Key idea — the production trade-off Instagram-scale serving means test-time compute is a product dial, not free lunch: latency budget per generation, compute cost per user, and quality all trade off. Expect questions like "how much test-time compute would you spend, and how do you decide per-request?" — answer with adaptive allocation: spend more when the critique model flags defects, less when the first sample passes.

§5 · Muse Video & the open problems Meta named

  • Same pretraining base as Muse Image — one multimodal foundation, two heads of capability. This is the strongest public hint about MSL's unified-stack strategy and a natural bridge to multimodal scaling-law discussion: how do you set the image/video/text data mixture when one base must serve all three?
  • Native audio — generated jointly, not dubbed on. Open problem #1: audio–video synchronization (lip-sync, contact sounds landing on the frame where contact happens).
  • Open problem #2: physically accurate fast motion — fast dynamics are exactly where frame-level pixel prediction is weakest, because appearance changes fast while the underlying dynamics stay low-dimensional.
◆ Frontier trick — your LatentFusion hook "Physically accurate fast motion" is a latent-dynamics argument: predicting in a representation space (video-JEPA style) rather than pixel space regularizes toward plausible dynamics instead of photometric detail. That is precisely your LatentFusion proposal — an LLM predicting in frozen video-JEPA latent space. When Muse Video's weaknesses come up, this is your opening to pitch it.

§6 · Content Seal — provenance watermarking

  • Invisible signal embedded in every generation; Meta claims robustness to cropping, compression, resizing, and screenshots, with a public detection tool.
  • Technical framing (inference): a watermark is a code embedded in perceptually-null directions; robustness = surviving the distortion channel (crop/resize/JPEG). The screenshot claim is the strong one — it implies robustness to display re-render + recapture, so the signal must live in low-frequency, geometry-tolerant features rather than pixel-exact patterns.
  • Eval question worth raising: robustness under adversarial removal (regeneration attacks — running the image through another model) vs benign transforms. Benign robustness is largely solved; adversarial is not.

§8 · Self-test

Answer out loud before revealing. These are phrased the way an interviewer would ask them.

Why does best-of-N sampling flatten while sequential refinement keeps scaling?▶
BoN draws i.i.d. samples and selects: $\mathbb{E}[\max] \approx \mu + \sigma\sqrt{2\ln N}$, so gains shrink like $\sqrt{\ln N}$ and are bounded by the base distribution's support — the model never gets better, you just search it. Sequential refinement conditions each attempt on the last attempt plus a critique, shifting the sampling distribution itself ($\mu_{t+1} > \mu_t$), giving $\approx a + b\ln C$ growth that isn't bounded by the base distribution. That's why Meta's "deliberate reasoning" beats BoN at matched compute.
Meta says self-refinement "emerged" from RL. Mechanistically, how does a behavior emerge without explicit supervision?▶
The rollout includes optional critique/edit actions in the action space. Occasionally the policy samples them; when a critique-then-edit trajectory scores higher reward than stopping early, its (group-relative) advantage is positive and every action in it — including the critique — gets upweighted. Repeated over training, the habit consolidates. Requirements: the action space must permit the behavior, exploration must occasionally hit it, and the reward must actually prefer refined outputs. Same mechanism as "wait, let me reconsider" emerging in reasoning-model RL.
How would you build a reward signal for image generation, where there's no ground truth?▶
Layer it: (1) learned preference/aesthetic RM from human pairwise data (Bradley–Terry fit); (2) instruction-faithfulness judges — a VLM checking countable claims (object counts, spatial relations, rendered text); (3) verifiable sub-rewards where tools apply (QR scans, plot matches data); (4) KL anchor to a reference policy to bound reward hacking. Then name the failure mode unprompted: preference RMs reward glossiness → style collapse; mitigate with RM ensembles, data refresh, and adversarial RM probing.
Why route text/charts/QR codes through a code tool instead of scaling the generator until it renders text correctly?▶
Exact symbolic structure (glyphs, axes, QR error-correction patterns) is a worst case for likelihood-trained decoders: errors are perceptually catastrophic but occupy tiny probability mass, so scaling attacks it inefficiently. A renderer is exactly correct by construction and its output re-enters the context as conditioning. Bonus: tool outputs are verifiable, giving clean reward signal in RL — you convert an unverifiable-reward problem into a partially verifiable one.
Muse Video's stated weaknesses are AV sync and fast motion. Why is fast motion specifically hard, and what's one research direction?▶
Fast motion maximizes pixel-space change per frame while the underlying state change stays low-dimensional — pixel-prediction objectives spend capacity on photometric detail rather than dynamics, and motion blur/large displacements break local correlations that convolutional/patch priors rely on. Direction: predict in a learned latent space (video-JEPA style) where dynamics are smooth and physics-relevant factors dominate, then decode — regularizing toward plausible dynamics instead of per-pixel fidelity. (Then pitch LatentFusion.)
Arena Elo ranks Muse Image #2. What are three reasons to distrust that number, and what would you add?▶
(1) Preference ≠ faithfulness: voters reward aesthetics; a model can rank high while following instructions worse. (2) Prompt-distribution bias: arena prompts are short/casual; production and professional use differ. (3) Elo is relative and non-stationary — it drifts as the model pool and voter pool change, and pairwise sampling isn't uniform. Add: instruction-faithfulness benchmarks with programmatic checks (counts, relations, text rendering), edit-locality metrics for editing, and stratified human evals by prompt category.
