PART I · Field Map — how modern VLLMs do "science of data"

Vision, pretraining, post-training — with the emphasis on multimodal data. Concepts first, then the hard-won truths.

I.1 · What "science of data" actually means

The discipline of turning data decisions from folklore ("more is better, dedup helps") into predictable, measured levers with laws attached. Four commitments:

1. Data is a design space, not a pile

Axes: quantity, quality, diversity, freshness, modality mix, difficulty, provenance. Each is a knob you can turn and measure.

2. Predictable before large

The goal is a function: given a recipe, predict frontier eval from small-scale runs. If you can't predict it, you don't understand it.

3. Marginal value, not average value

Never "is captions data good" — always "what is the marginal gain per token of captions given everything already in the mix." Data value is contextual and saturating.

4. Compute-optimal allocation

Under a fixed budget, split across modalities, quality tiers, and PT-vs-post — the multimodal generalization of Chinchilla.

One-sentence thesis: convert the token budget into maximum capability per FLOP by discovering the mixture, curriculum, and quality function that generalize from proxy scale to frontier scale — and express the answer as laws, not vibes.

I.2b · The interleaved pipeline — from raw crawl to training sequence

The "interleaved docs" row above is manufactured by a specific pipeline (MMC4 / OBELICS / OmniCorpus lineage). The whole game is preserving where each image sits in the document flow — that positional structure is the thing alt-text pairs don't have, and it's what teaches in-context multimodal ability (Flamingo's M3W was the proof).

  1. Acquire — WARC, never WET. Common Crawl ships two formats: WET (pre-extracted text — images already gone, useless here) and WARC (raw HTTP responses with full HTML). Start from WARC, or run your own crawler (URL frontier, robots.txt, per-domain politeness). URL-level triage first, it's nearly free: domain blocklists (adult/spam/SEO farms), URL dedup across snapshots, fastText language ID.
  2. Parse — the interleaving is born here. HTML → DOM tree; main-content detection strips nav/ads/comments/footers while preserving node order; a reading-order DOM walk emits the skeleton [text, text, IMG, text, IMG, …]. Contrast: MMC4 had to reconstruct interleaving post-hoc by CLIP-matching images to C4 sentences (positions were already lost); OBELICS/OmniCorpus keep DOM order natively — strictly better provenance.
  3. Image path. Resolve real image URLs (srcset variants, lazy-load attributes — naive src misses a large fraction); fleet-scale fetch with politeness and retries (expect ~30–40% dead links); decode-validate; content-hash everything. Then image filters: min-size/aspect-ratio (kills icons and banner ads), NSFW + CSAM hash matching (non-negotiable), ad/logo heuristics, EXIF stripping.
  4. Document path. Text-quality rules (Gopher-style stats, perplexity filters), bounds on images-per-doc and image:text ratio (a page with 40 images and 50 words is a gallery or a shop, not a document), spam/SEO scoring.
  5. Dedup + decontamination. Text: MinHash-LSH near-dup at doc level. Images: pHash + embedding-NN — and eval decontamination in image space (Project A's P3: the same benchmark figure arrives as screenshot, crop, PDF render, re-encode; text dedup is blind to all of them).
  6. Assembly + alignment scoring. Re-attach surviving images at their DOM positions; score CLIP(image, surrounding text) to drop off-topic stragglers (ads that survived heuristics); doc-level coherence gate. Dropping an image ≠ dropping the doc — the text keeps its slot order.
  7. Serialize. Tokenize with <image> placeholders pointing at image tensors; pack docs into sequences under a per-sequence image budget; shard (WebDataset/ArrayRecord); the family enters the mixture with its own weight and its own release gates (stats dashboards: images/doc, tokens/doc, dedup rates; then a proxy-run decision check).
# Attrition is the defining statistic — the funnel is brutal:
#   OBELICS: ~25 CC snapshots → 141M docs · 353M images · 115B tokens  (≈1% of candidate pages survive)
#   OmniCorpus: same recipe industrialized → 2.2B docs · 8.6B images (multilingual, multi-source)
# Every stage's reject rate is a *tunable quality knob* — and per I.2's taxonomy row,
# the payoff is capacity-gated: near-zero at 300M, strongly positive at ≥3B.

The speakable summary: "Start from WARC because WET already threw the images away; extract with the DOM walk so interleaving is inherited, not reconstructed; run images and text down parallel filter paths; dedup and decontaminate in image space; re-attach and CLIP-gate images at their original slots; pack with placeholders under an image budget. The funnel keeps ~1% — and every reject threshold is a mixture-quality knob you can put a scaling law on."

I.3 · Vision-encoder data science

The encoder sets the ceiling of perception; no post-training recovers detail the encoder discarded. It's the highest-leverage, least-reversible data decision.

  • Contrastive (CLIP/SigLIP): needs massive diverse pairs; improves as a power law in unique pairs (dedup shifts the curve left); learns aligned but bag-of-words-ish semantics.
  • Caption/generative pretraining: needs denser, grammatical text; teaches attributes/relations contrastive misses. Re-captioning web data with a strong VLM is among the highest-ROI interventions known (MetaCLIP-2: iteratively human-refined captioner + ~15% synthetic-caption sampling).
  • Self-supervised (DINO/MAE/JEPA): no text needed; spatially rich features. Frontier move: fuse SSL + contrastive targets (→ my LatentFusion line).
  • Resolution is a data-budget decision: a 4-tile 896px doc image costs ~4× a thumbnail. The resolution schedule — which content earns high-res tokens — has its own compute-optimal frontier per task family, and it's where DocVQA/ChartQA gains live.

I.4 · Pretraining-data science PT

The mixture problem (the central object)

Given sources S₁..Sₙ, weights w (Σw=1), budget T:
  w* = argmax_w Σ_task λ_task · Eval_task(mix(w))   s.t. availability caps
Intractable to sweep at scale → replace the sweep with PREDICTORS:
proxy-model regressions, data-mixing laws, gradient/influence attribution.

The four sub-disciplines

  • Modality balance & interference: push image tokens too high and text reasoning regresses. It's a Pareto front (text ↔ vision), managed as a constrained optimization with a text floor — and interference turns out to be sparse (§I.7.4).
  • Curriculum & staging: text warmup → ramp pairs → interleaved → video/audio → quality-anneal the final ~15% (upweight cleanest/hardest data under fast LR decay — the biggest lever discovered in recent years; OLMo-2/Llama-3/Phi-4 all do versions of it).
  • Quality/dedup/decontamination: per-modality quality classifiers; near-dup across modalities (image hash + text minhash); eval decontamination in image space, because the same figure appears as PDF, HTML, blog re-post, screenshot, crop, and re-captioned copy — four "different" samples, one concept.
  • Attribution: leave-one-out proxy runs (gold standard) > influence functions/TRAK (cheap prefilter only) > gradient alignment (DoReMi/DoGE) > datamodels. Rule: never ship a recipe change on approximate attribution alone.

I.5 · Post-training-data science Post

  • SFT: coverage beats volume. Past a few hundred K examples, more-of-the-same adds little; what matters is the skill × modality × format coverage matrix (counting, OCR, charts, spatial, multi-image, refusal, tools). Science = measure the grid, fill the holes.
  • Synthetic engines & the collapse boundary: generate with model+tools (renderers, code), keep only verifier-passed samples — the filter is the product. Watch embedding-coverage and entropy; cap the synthetic fraction; keep synthetic as rare-skill filler, never bulk.
  • RL/preference: the crown-jewel MM problem is hallucination — reward image-grounded honesty. RLVR extends naturally via programmatically-checkable visual tasks (rendered charts, counting, OCR exact-match, geometry).
  • The PT↔post split: a capability like chart reasoning can be bought at four stages (PT, anneal, SFT, RL) with different cost curves. Most post-training "gains" are elicitation of PT-latent skills — so never try to install raw perception in SFT.

I.6 · Measurement & infrastructure (the unglamorous half)

Eval that isn't noise

Low-variance, decontaminated, capability-decomposed suites; CIs on every claim. Data experiments live or die here.

Provenance & lineage

Every token traceable to source, license, filter version. Without lineage: no attribution, no reproduction, no science.

Proxy-model infra

The lab bench. The whole science is bottlenecked by proxy runs/day — treat throughput as a first-class deliverable.

Counterfactual bookkeeping

"We added X and eval went up" is not evidence unless X was the only change. Culture, not tooling.

I.7 · Deep insights — the hard-won truths beneath the taxonomy

The layer that separates "has read the papers" from "has burned the compute." Each is a defensible claim with its mechanism.

7.1 Loss is not capability, and the gap is structured

Stream loss improves smoothly; capabilities ride on top through mostly-sigmoidal link functions — "emergence" is usually a sharp link over smooth loss. The deep part: different capabilities sit at different points on their sigmoids at any scale, so the same intervention looks transformative for one eval and useless for another even when it moved both losses equally. Misreading link position as data value is the field's most common analytic error.

Eval_e(N, D, w) ≈ σ_e( L_s(N, D, w) )        with   σ_e(L) = h_e / (1 + exp( k_e·(L − L₀_e) )) + floor_e
· L₀_e : the "threshold loss" where capability e turns on   · k_e : link sharpness ("emergence" = large k)
· ∂Eval/∂(data) = σ'_e(L) · ∂L/∂(data)  →  the SAME data intervention is scaled by σ', which
  varies over orders of magnitude across evals at any fixed scale. That's the whole trap.

7.2 Data value is a derivative, and derivatives don't commute

Marginal value of X is ∂L/∂w_x at the current mixture. Two honest labs get opposite signs on the same source because they evaluate the derivative at different operating points — which is also why published ablations transfer so poorly: they're derivatives at undisclosed base mixes.

7.3 The best data is the data the model almost understands

Training value peaks at the edge of current competence: too easy ⇒ no gradient information; too hard ⇒ noise. This one principle explains why quality multipliers grow with N, why curriculum falls out of the fitted laws, and why quality filtering can go negative at scale (DataComp-VLM): the filter deletes the hard-but-learnable band along with the garbage. Think moving competence frontier, not fixed quality bar.

7.4 Interference is sparse, and that's a gift

"Vision hurts text" is folk wisdom; measurement localizes interference in a few source×stream pairs (high-volume low-density data crowding its own modality's dense data; format-heavy data bleeding format into free generation). Sparse ⇒ fixable by surgery (cap one family), not retreat (lower all image weight). Labs that believe the folk version leave capability on the table permanently.

7.5 MM dedup is a semantic problem wearing an infrastructure costume

The same concept arrives as photo, crop, re-encode, screenshot, PDF figure, re-captioned copy, and model-generated description. Hashes catch the first three; nothing standard catches the rest — which is exactly how contamination and value double-counting happen. Uncomfortable conclusion: your unique-token count U — the denominator of every scaling claim — is soft, in the direction that flatters you.

7.6 The anneal works because of what it displaces, not just what it adds

The anneal window is zero-sum: every gold token displaces a base-mix token at the moment of highest learning-rate-per-token. In careful ablations, a surprising fraction of the "anneal effect" is the removal of noisy data from the decay window — anneal-on-filtered-base-mix (no gold at all) captures much of the lift. This changes what you buy: cleaning the schedule's tail is cheaper than manufacturing gold.

7.7 Every synthetic pipeline is secretly a distillation of its judge

Generator proposes, verifier disposes — so the surviving distribution carries the judge's biases at compound interest (each data-engine generation re-selects for them). Invest in judge diversity and adversarial judge-evals with the same seriousness as generator quality: the judge is the actual teacher.

7.8 Mixture weights are a lie unless you audit realized tokens

You set w over documents; the model experiences tokens. Document lengths differ 100× across families, packing splits images across sequence boundaries, loss-masking changes which tokens carry gradient. Nominal w and realized gradient-token share routinely differ by 2×. A law fit on nominal weights is fit on fiction — this one accounting discipline invalidates a good fraction of public mixture research.

7.9 The eval suite is part of the training recipe

Whatever you measure, the optimizer maximizes. Saturated evals add noise; unrepresented capabilities silently decay; contaminated evals steer data selection toward more contamination. Treat the suite as a versioned, headroom-weighted, decontaminated component of the recipe, re-derived each cycle — because last cycle's optimization saturated last cycle's suite.

7.10 The half-life of a data law is one model generation

Exponents, q multipliers, transfer terms are conditioned on architecture, tokenizer, context, and (for synthetic data) the generator. The durable assets are never coefficients; they are the instrument — proxy ladders, calibration gates, realized-token audits, decontamination pipelines — and the functional forms. Teams that treasure coefficients ossify; teams that treasure instruments compound.

Carry five: loss≠capability (7.1) · value is a derivative at your operating point (7.2) · quality is a moving competence frontier (7.3) · audit realized tokens (7.8) · the asset is the instrument, not the coefficients (7.10).

Project A · MixOracle — a predictive mixture optimizer for native-MM pretraining PT~9 months, team of 5

A1 · Problem & my role

Problem. The pretraining mixture (~40 sources, 8 families) was hand-tuned by committee; nobody could answer "what happens to ChartQA if we halve interleaved and double OCR?" without a frontier-scale gamble. Charter: make the recipe a prediction, not a negotiation.

My role: technical lead of the mixture-modeling workstream (1 of 3 workstreams, ~5 people). Personally: designed the proxy ladder and its calibration gate, derived and implemented the capacity-aware mixing-law fit, owned the constrained optimizer, wrote the recipe recommendation the pretraining lead signed. Did not own data pipelines (partner team) or the eval harness (consumed it; drove one major change — problem #6). Two REs ran sweeps; I designed every sweep and analyzed every result.

A2 · Methodology, iterated honestly

v1 — "DoReMi it" (failed usefully, ~6 weeks)

Group-DRO with a 300M proxy. Produced weights, but: (1) DRO's worst-case excess-loss objective pathologically upweighted the noisiest OCR shards (high reducible loss ≠ high capability value); (2) black box — no counterfactuals without re-running. Carried lesson: I didn't want a weight-producer, I wanted a differentiable model of the recipe space.

v2 — explicit mixing laws (the workhorse)

Ŝ_e(w) = c_e + a_e·exp(Σ_i b_{e,i}·w_i)     # per-eval exponential mixing law
w* = argmax_w Σ_e λ_e Ŝ_e(w)  s.t. Σw=1, w_i≤cap_i, Ŝ_text(w)≥floor

~60 proxy runs at Latin-hypercube weights; held-out R²=0.93 at 1B. Then the 3B check exposed rank inversion on reasoning-heavy evals — the 1B optimum underweighted dense/interleaved data that only pays with capacity (the failure mode capacity-aware mixture laws later formalized).

v3 — capacity-aware + rank-gated (what shipped)

  • N as an explicit axis: joint fit over (w, N) at 300M/1B/3B; coefficients allowed to flip sign with scale — interleaved docs did (mildly negative at 300M → strongly positive at 3B).
  • Calibration gate: a proxy rung is trusted per-eval only if its ranking over 12 probe mixtures hits Spearman ρ≥0.8 vs the 8B reference. The 300M rung failed for reasoning evals and was excluded for those evals only.
  • Video pre-solved off-model: dose–response showed eval-per-token is dominated by tokens-per-clip, not clip count → optimized the frame/token policy separately, entered the mixture solve as a constant. Dimensionality reduction by problem decomposition.
  • Extrapolate → verify: solve w* at target N; one frontier run; residuals tracked as the pipeline's own quality metric.

A3 · Problem bank — everything that actually bit (8 problems)

P1 · Rank inversion: the proxy lied at scale ▶

Small models are capacity-bottlenecked, so noisy high-volume data "looks" great at 1B; at 8B, dense/hard data pays. Fixes: fit on rank vs a larger reference (the ρ≥0.8 gate), add N as a law axis and extrapolate the optimum in N, weight proxy evals toward capacity-gated capabilities. Proxy design — making small runs predictive of large ones — is the hardest, most valuable skill in the field.

P2 · Eval variance drowned the signal ▶

Run-to-run noise (±2–3 pts) matched the effect sizes. Fixes: cluster ~30 evals into 11 capability clusters (halves σ), multi-seed at the cheapest rung to measure the noise floor, common data order across arms so paired deltas are low-variance, claims as slopes across dose sweeps rather than two-point diffs, bootstrapped CIs on everything. The measured σ doubles as the Huber δ in the fit — one measurement, two uses. The objective itself was also part of the fix — headroom weighting, so saturated evals stop injecting noise:

λ_e ∝ (ceiling_e − current_e) · strategic_e     recomputed quarterly
# variance decomposition that won the argument: ~70% of aggregate movement
# across proxy arms was seed noise from 3 saturated evals — the objective
# was mostly optimizing noise until the reweight.
P3 · Silent contamination through re-captioning ▶

A suspiciously good DocVQA number: our re-captioning model had seen benchmark images in its training, and its captions leaked answers into our synthetic source — pixels matched a test image while text was freshly generated, so text dedup was blind. Fixes: decontaminate in image space (perceptual hash + embedding-NN, cosine ≥0.95 vs all eval images) before captioning; rule: any synthetic source inherits the contamination set of its generator's training data. The gain shrank to its honest size.

P4 · Cross-modal interference: text regressed ▶

The eval-maximizing mix quietly cost ~1.5 pts of pure-text reasoning — invisible on MM dashboards. Fixes: text floor as a hard constraint; fitted cross-terms localized the interference to one high-volume low-density pair family; capping it alone recovered text at near-zero MM cost. Interference is a few bad sources, not "vision vs language."

P5 · The optimizer chased the fit's blind spots at the simplex boundary ▶

Great fit in the interior; the solve pushed to the boundary (max OCR, max re-captioned) — exactly where proxy coverage was thinnest. Fixes: trust-region active sampling (~15 runs re-allocated near the proposed w*, refit) plus an epistemic penalty — maximize Ŝ − κ·bootstrap-std(Ŝ). The optimum moved ~30% relative in two families. Optimize-into-your-model's-blind-spot is the default failure of model-based optimization; budget for the second round from day one.

P6 · Multi-epoch reality: the law had no concept of repetition ▶

Gold families were capped — at target budget they'd repeat 4–8 epochs, and repeated-token value decays. Fix: effective-token discount D_eff,i = U_i·(1−r_i^{e_i})/(1−r_i) with per-family r fit from 1/2/4/8-epoch branches. The answer changed materially: the optimizer stopped over-allocating to small gold sets and instead flagged unique gold tokens as the binding constraint — the formal justification that funded Project B. The two projects connect exactly here.

P7 · Attribution disagreed with dose–response ▶

Influence functions flagged a source as high-value; a clean leave-one-out run showed ~zero. Trust hierarchy: LOO/add-in runs are ground truth; influence estimates are a prefilter for ranking candidates, confirmed by real ablations before any recipe change. First-order influence is unreliable under strong mixing nonlinearity — never ship on it alone.

P8 · A pipeline change silently invalidated 25 sweep points — and stakeholders kept lobbying ▶

Two organizational failure modes with one root cause: uncontrolled change. A partner team's improved OCR filter changed the distribution under an unchanged source name (caught by a residual trend vs launch date); teams lobbied to inject pet corpora mid-flight. Fixes: recipe pinning — immutable snapshot IDs + filter versions as the law's covariates; and an evidentiary bar — any new source enters via standard dose–response + mix-in runs and earns weight only if its marginal value clears CI. Turned "who lobbies hardest" into "what does the curve say" — honestly half the project's value.

A4 · Results

  • Frontier eval vector within CI on 9 of 11 clusters; both misses (long-video, multi-image) were long-context-sensitive — anticipated, flagged, became next-cycle work.
  • Beat the hand-tuned incumbent at iso-FLOP on the headroom-weighted aggregate, pure-text within guardrail.
  • Standing artifact: recipe counterfactuals answered in minutes from the fitted surface, with CIs — the cultural deliverable.

Project B · CapForge — re-captioning + quality-tier engine, delivered through the anneal Data engine + mid-training~6 months, team of 4

B1 · Problem & my role

Problem. A's law said unique high-density caption tokens were the binding constraint. The literature agreed on the shape of the answer: MetaCLIP-2-style iterative re-captioning; Molmo/PixMo-style human dense captions (speech-first, ~200 words) setting the ceiling. Charter: manufacture the missing gold tokens, prove their value causally, deliver them where they pay most.

My role: initiated and led it (my A-P6 finding justified it). Personally: designed the captioner-improvement loop and annotation spec, defined the tier rubric and tier-classifier recipe, ran the anneal experiments end-to-end, made the two decisive calls below. Annotation ops and inference fleet were partner-owned.

B2 · The engine, iterated (re-scoped once mid-flight)

v1 — captioner bootstrap loop

  1. Seed: speech-first human dense captions (annotators talk 60–90s per image; ASR + normalization) — speaking produces density typing never does, and it's distillation-free.
  2. Iterate: train captioner → caption 50M images → humans edit a sample (edit distance = quality metric and new training signal) → ~3 rounds. Refine the captioner, not the corpus.
  3. Tier: distilled judge buckets everything: T0 human/edited · T1 model, judge-passed · T2 model, unverified · T3 raw alt-text.

v2 — the re-scope: from "filter harder" to "mix smarter"

The plan said drop T3 aggressively. Two findings killed that: our dose–response showed T3 carries irreplaceable long-tail entity coverage, and DataComp-VLM's headline landed simultaneously — mixing, not filtering, is the dominant lever; filters can go negative. v2 keeps every tier and optimizes the tier ratio, including MetaCLIP-2-style ~15% synthetic-caption sampling against original alt-text for the same image (clean and wild registers of the same visual content).

v3 — delivery via anneal

2×2 at proxy scale: {gold in base mix vs final-15% anneal} × {fast vs standard LR decay}. Anneal+fast-decay dominated: same tokens, ~2.3× the lift. Final recipe: T0/T1 concentrated in the anneal window with a reserved general-data ratio (prevents backward regression); T2/T3 carry the base mix.

B3 · Problem bank (4 problems)

P1 · Judge and captioner shared a lineage — self-preference corrupted the tiers ▶

T1 acceptance climbed every round; a blinded human audit said quality was flat — the judge increasingly preferred its sibling's style. Fixes: re-anchor the judge each round on human edit distance; adversarial probes (captions with planted errors — wrong count, wrong color, invented object) with a planted-error-recall gate on every judge release; triangulate judge ∧ OCR-consistency ∧ alignment score, disagreements → human audit. Acceptance "progress" flattened to honest levels — the correct outcome.

P2 · House-style diversity collapse ▶

By round 3, captions converged on a template ("The image shows… In the foreground…"); corpus n-gram entropy fell every round; downstream models described everything in one register. Fixes: retrain the captioner to emit k registers per image (terse inventory / dense spatial / OCR-focused / narrative), sample register at training time; keep alt-text sampling as a diversity reservoir; make n-gram entropy and embedding dispersion release-gating metrics for every data drop.

P3 · Anneal gains that didn't survive post-training ▶

Clean base-model gains; half the delta vanished after SFT+RL on chat evals. Diagnosis first: probe evals splitting perception (likelihood/forced-choice — format-free) from expression (chat format). Perception survived fully; expression was overwritten by SFT data less dense than our anneal data. Fix was downstream: regenerate a slice of SFT responses to inherit CapForge density. Rule shipped org-wide: an anneal gain isn't real until it survives your own post-training pipeline.

P4 · Fleet economics: is the 8× cheaper captioner 8× worse? ▶

Ran it as science: dose–response of downstream eval vs captioner tier at fixed tokens. Clean knee — the distilled captioner kept ~90% of value at ~15% of cost except on OCR-dense/fine-grained images. Shipped a router: cheap by default, big model for a trivially-detectable hard slice. ~70% cost cut at ~97% of value. Also: fleet cost lived in the tail (mega-resolution images, 100-object scenes) — a caption-length cap and a downscale pass for the top-1% resolution tail cut another 25%.

B4 · The autorater recipe — how the caption judge is actually built

B1–B3 use the judge everywhere (tiering, planted-error gates, P1's self-preference fix) but the recipe deserves its own answer, because "how do I create an autorater for caption quality?" is a question I should be able to whiteboard end-to-end.

Step 0 — the contract before any model: an autorater is defined by the decision it drives, not the score it emits. Filter/tier a billion captions → needs throughput + a threshold that's stable across styles. Rank captioners or pick best-of-k → needs pairwise reliability. Reward for RL/DPO → needs robustness to being optimized against (the hardest contract). Regression-gate a new captioner release → needs sensitivity to small deltas. Pick the contract first; it sets the architecture, the calibration bar, and the cost budget.

Step 1 · Decompose "quality" — never ask for a 1–10

A single scalar is noisy, miscalibrated, and hides the one tradeoff that matters: longer captions gain coverage and lose faithfulness. Score axes separately: precision (every claim true of the image — hallucination is 1−P), recall (salient content mentioned: objects, attributes, relations, counts, text-in-image), density (information per token), fluency/register compliance. VLM judges are much more reliable on binary atomic checks than on holistic Likert ratings — so make everything a binary check and aggregate.

Step 2 · The core mechanism: claim decomposition + VQA verification

# Precision side (hallucination):
#   LLM decomposes caption → atomic claims  {"a red car", "car left of tree", "sign reads 'OPEN'", "3 people"}
#   strong VLM verifies each claim against the image as binary VQA
#   P = #verified / #claims                       (per-type: object | attribute | relation | count | OCR)
# Recall side (coverage):
#   build an image inventory INDEPENDENT of the candidate: detector + OCR outputs,
#   or claims from human/reference captions, or the union of k diverse model captions
#   R = #inventory items covered by caption / #inventory
# Combine per the contract:  F_β = (1+β²)PR/(β²P+R)   — β>1 for dense-caption training data,
# but REPORT P and R separately; the scalar is for thresholding only.

This is the FaithScore / Davidsonian-scene-graph pattern. The independence of the recall inventory is the load-bearing detail — if the inventory comes from the same model family as the captions, coverage grades its own homework. Note CLIPScore's role: a gate, not a grade — it's bag-of-words-ish, saturates on dense captions, and is blind to compositional and counting errors.

Step 3 · Tier the compute; Step 4 · pairwise mode for ranking

Throughput: frontier judge on everything is unaffordable at 10⁸–10⁹ scale. Distill: label 1–5M captions with the full claim-level pipeline, train a small scorer (regression head, or per-axis heads) on those labels, keep the frontier pipeline for audits, disagreement escalation, and re-labeling each generation — insight 7.7 applies: the corpus becomes a distillation of this judge, so the judge's blind spots become the data's blind spots. Ranking contract: use pairwise A/B judgments (swap order and average — position bias is real), aggregate with Bradley–Terry: p(A≻B)=σ(r_A−r_B); pairwise is markedly more human-consistent than absolute scores for close calls.

Step 5 · Validation — the judge is a model and gets a model's eval

  • Gold set: 500–1k images with expert error annotations (or human edit-distance labels, as in v1). Metric: agreement with human majority vs the human–human agreement ceiling — a judge at 92% of a 95% ceiling is done; chasing 99% is fitting annotator noise.
  • Planted-error suite (the CapForge P1 gate): mutate known-good captions — swap a color, count ±1, invent an object, flip a spatial relation, corrupt the OCR string — judge must catch ≥ threshold per error type. Every judge release re-runs this; it's the judge's unit test.
  • Calibration audits: score-vs-length curve must be flat for equal-quality captions (length bias), per-register score distributions compared (style bias), and sibling-model outputs spot-audited by humans (self-preference — P1's failure was exactly this).
  • Decision-level validation, the only one that counts: train on rater-selected vs random-selected data at proxy scale — does the rater's selection beat random at equal tokens? A judge that correlates with humans but doesn't move downstream evals is measuring something real and irrelevant.

PART III · Scaling-Law Mechanics — how the laws were actually developed

Data, model size, and learning rate. The layer for "okay, but how exactly did you fit it?"

III.1 · The four-layer stack (the order is the method)

  1. Tuning law — optimal LR/batch/schedule vs N and D. Skip it and every larger model is differently mistuned: "the scaling law" becomes a law of your mistuning.
  2. Base compute law — L(N, D) on a fixed distribution. Calibrates the instrument on the easiest problem.
  3. Data law — D promoted from a count to count × composition × quality × repetition.
  4. MM joint law — per-modality budgets and weights as first-class variables with interaction terms.
I spent ~2 months on layers 1–2 with zero multimodal content. It looked slow; it's what made layers 3–4 fast and trustworthy. Every scaling-law disaster I know is someone fitting layer 4 with layer 1 broken.

III.2 · Base law: L(N, D), three methods, one acceptance test

L(N,D) = E + A/N^α + B/D^β                     (Chinchilla form)
E: irreducible loss = data-distribution entropy — differs PER MODALITY STREAM
   (image-conditioned text < free text; OCR tokens lower still)

Full compute-optimal derivation (be able to do this on a whiteboard):
  minimize L(N,D)  s.t.  C = 6ND   →  substitute D = C/6N:
  L(N) = E + A·N^{-α} + B·(6N/C)^{β}
  dL/dN = −αA·N^{-α-1} + βB·6^{β}·N^{β-1}·C^{-β} = 0
  ⇒ N^{α+β} = (αA / βB6^β) · C^{β}
  ⇒ N* = G · C^{β/(α+β)} ,   D* = C/(6N*) ∝ C^{α/(α+β)}
  ⇒ a = β/(α+β),  b = α/(α+β),  a+b = 1  (always — a useful sanity check)
With our fits (α≈0.32, β≈0.29): a≈0.475, b≈0.525 → near-proportional N–D
scaling, tokens slightly favored — Chinchilla-adjacent, slightly data-hungry.
Loss along the optimal frontier:  L*(C) − E ∝ C^{−αβ/(α+β)}  (≈ C^{−0.152})
— the exponent that answers "what does 10× compute buy?": ×10 compute ⇒
loss gap to E shrinks by 10^0.152 ≈ 1.42×.
MethodHowTrust level
M1 · Fixed-N curvesOne long run per N; read L(D) along the curveCheapest, but mid-curve points are off-schedule (LR not decayed for that budget) → systematically pessimistic; the root of Kaplan's undertrained optima

First pass failed it — traced to the LR confound (III.3) that M3 alone would have silently absorbed into biased exponents.

Bookkeeping that changes exponents: count non-embedding N (with a 256K MM vocab, embeddings are ~35% of params at 150M vs ~4% at 8B — the small end bends the fit); use exact FLOPs at long context (C≈6ND ignores attention — video bites); freeze the val battery and tokenizer for the entire sweep.

III.3 · LR & batch laws — and why WSD changed the economics

  • μP as backbone, sweeps as audit. Optimal base-LR ≈ width-invariant under μP; tune once small, transfer across width. Verified empirically at 3 widths — held for the text tower, but the vision patch-embed path violated μP's input-statistics assumptions and needed its own multiplier. Without it, large models were mistuned on image tokens and the image-loss exponent was biased. Trust, with a receipt.
  • Residual corrections: η*(N,D) = η₀·N^{-γ_N}·D^{γ_D} on top of μP (γ's small but nonzero).
  • Batch below the critical noise scale everywhere, so batch never enters the law as a confound.
μP in three lines (what actually changes vs standard parametrization):
  init:      W ~ N(0, 1/fan_in)              (hidden layers)
  LR:        η_layer = η_base / fan_in        (hidden);  η_base (input/bias)
  output:    logits scaled by 1/width
  ⇒ feature-learning updates stay Θ(1) as width→∞ ⇒ η*_base ≈ width-invariant
  Our exception: the vision patch-embed projection sees raw pixel statistics,
  not width-scaled activations — its effective fan-in assumption breaks ⇒ one
  extra tuned multiplier m_patch (we fit m_patch ≈ 4–8× the μP default).

Critical batch via gradient-noise scale (McCandlish et al.):
  B_crit ≈ tr(Σ) / |g|²      Σ = per-example gradient covariance, g = true gradient
  B ≪ B_crit: perfect scaling (halve steps by doubling batch, same tokens)
  B ≫ B_crit: token-inefficient (extra tokens buy nothing)
  MM subtlety: B_crit differs per stream (image-conditioned gradients are less
  noisy than free-text) → the binding constraint is the noisiest stream in the mix.
  We measured B_crit at 3 sizes and kept global batch ≤ ~½ of the smallest
  stream's B_crit — so batch never confounds the law.
WSD (warmup–stable–decay) vs cosine:
cosine: LR must END at the token budget ⇒ one run = ONE valid (N,D,L) point.
WSD: long stable trunk at η_max; branch a cheap decay (~10–20% cost) at D₁,D₂,D₃…
     ⇒ one trunk = MANY on-schedule points.  Run matrix ~4× cheaper.
Bonus: the decay branch IS the anneal window ⇒ the same branching infra served
       MixOracle's L(D) points AND CapForge's anneal experiments.
Caveat: NEVER mix trunk (undecayed) readings into the fit — the decay drop is
       large and mildly N-dependent. Decayed endpoints only.

III.4 · Data enters the law: repetition & quality

D_eff = Σ_t q_t(N) · U_t · (1 − r_t^{e_t}) / (1 − r_t)
r (repetition decay, per family): ≈0.55–0.7; ~4 epochs near-free, ~0 past ~16
  (Muennighoff-consistent). Dense captions tolerate MORE epochs than alt-text;
  grounding decays FASTEST (format memorization).
q (quality multiplier): a VECTOR, not a scalar — gold captions ≈3× on perception
  streams, ≈1× on text — and mildly N-dependent (+15–20% from 1B→3B: bigger
  models extract more from dense data → capacity-aware mixtures, curriculum).

How q is identified (the tier-swap estimator):
  Train pairs at FIXED total budget: arm₁ uses D tokens of tier-t data where
  arm₀ uses D tokens of reference-tier; measure ΔL_s. Then q_t solves
      B_s/(q_t·D)^{β_s} − B_s/(1·D)^{β_s} = ΔL_s
  i.e. q is defined as "the token-exchange rate that explains the loss delta
  under the already-fitted law" — so q estimates inherit the base law's CI,
  and a q fit before the base law converges is meaningless (layer order again).

III.5 · The MM joint law (the centerpiece)

Decision 1 · "Loss" is a vector

Per-stream validation losses — L_txt, L_img→txt, L_interleave, L_ocr, L_vid→txt — each on a frozen battery. Streams have different entropy floors E; a scalar "MM loss" is a category error. The single most consequential choice of the project: it made E identifiable, interference measurable, links stable, and the headline result visible.

Decision 2 · "D" for an image is a choice

One canonical tokenization for the whole sweep (256 tokens per 448px tile, ≤4 tiles + thumbnail); resolution policy studied separately, later. Mixing resolution changes into the sweep would have made D_img meaningless.

L_s(N, D, w) = E_s + A_s/N^{α_s} + Σ_i B_{s,i}/(q_i·D_eff,i)^{β_{s,i}}
               + Σ_{i≠j} γ_{s,ij}·f(w_i,w_j)        ← group-lasso'd cross-terms
Empirical structure that made it fittable:
· α differs by stream: α_txt≈0.32 vs α_img→txt≈0.24 — vision more DATA-limited
· cross-terms are SPARSE (interference = a few pairs; transfer = a few positive
  terms, e.g. text-math → chart stream)
· Evals via fitted sigmoidal links: Eval_e ≈ σ_e(L_s) — smooth laws in loss
  space, capability jumps mostly explained by sharp-but-fixed links
The constrained solve, formally (what "optimize the mixture" actually is):
  max_w  Σ_e λ_e·σ_e(L_s(N*,D,w)) − κ·û(w)          û = bootstrap std (epistemic penalty)
  s.t.   Σᵢwᵢ = 1;  0 ≤ wᵢ ≤ capᵢ;  σ_text(L_txt(w)) ≥ S_floor;  w_syn ≤ ρ(N)
KKT structure gives the interpretable readout leadership actually used:
  · interior families:  equal marginal value  ∂Obj/∂wᵢ = μ  (shared shadow price)
  · cap-bound families (OCR): ∂Obj/∂wᵢ > μ  → the GAP prices what more supply
    is worth → literally the CapForge funding number ("a marginal unique gold
    token is worth X× a marginal web-pair token at the frontier")
  · floor-bound (text): its multiplier prices what MM capability we pay for
    the last point of text protection — the cost of the guardrail, made visible
The two headline findings: (1) near-proportional N–D scaling held for the native-MM model (Apple-NMM-consistent) — the interesting deviations were all in D's composition, not its total; (2) w* moves with compute — because β and q vary by family and q grows with N, noisy pairs' share should fall and dense/interleaved rise along the frontier. A static mixture is optimal at exactly one scale; curriculum and anneal fall out of the same fitted surface.

III.7 · The confounds that nearly sank it

C1 · Optimal LR shifted with the mixture — tuning and data laws are coupled ▶

Image-heavy vs text-heavy mixtures had ~1.5–2× different η* at fixed N (different gradient-noise statistics per stream). My "tune first, then sweep data" separation was leaky: far-from-reference mixtures were mistuned, and the mixing-law coefficients absorbed the mistuning — "the value of image data" partly measured "how mistuned image-heavy runs were." Fix: measure ∂η*/∂w at 6 mixture corners, apply the correction (a function of image-token fraction), re-run ~10 worst arms. Coefficient shifts up to 20%. The LR law is part of the data law.

C2 · Loss vs evals — which does the law predict? ▶

Loss was smooth and law-like; counting/OCR-exact-match moved in jumps. Two-stage answer: laws live in loss space; a per-eval sigmoidal link σ_e, fit once across all runs, converts predictions. Links are stable because loss is a sufficient statistic for most variation. Most "emergence" = sharp-but-fixed link over smooth loss; the two evals that stayed unpredictable were flagged as genuine risk, not hidden.

C3 · Per-source validation sets — self-graded homework ▶

Early val sets were drawn per-source, so mixture changes changed the test distribution per arm — the law was grading its own homework, and the bug makes curves smoother and prettier, not uglier. Fix: freeze a mixture-independent battery (fixed sets per stream, frozen tokenizer, frozen tiling) before any fitting, versioned like code. The most common silent killer of data-law projects.

C4 · E–β identifiability: two stories, one fit ▶

On streams that never reached low loss (video), bootstrap showed huge E↔β covariance: "high floor, fast decay" and "low floor, slow decay" fit identically but imply opposite frontier decisions. Fixes: buy identifiability where cheap (extra-long branches on small N pin the D→∞ behavior); where it can't be bought, report the CI region and decline to allocate — conservative prior instead. A law that knows what it doesn't know is the difference between science and curve-fitting theater.

IV.3 · Trench details — "only doers know"

Dataloader truths

  • Nominal vs realized: weights are set over documents; the model sees tokens. Doc lengths differ 100×, packing splits images, loss-masking drops tokens — realized gradient-token share was off nominal w by up to 2.3×. Everything refit on loader-logged realized counts. Fastest tell of a trustworthy mixture paper: which one do they fit on?
  • Determinism under resumption: checkpoint-resume reshuffled shard order, so preempted arms saw different data — unblinded noise in exactly the longest runs. Fixing it cut arm-to-arm σ ~30%.
  • The 4-tile bug: portrait images silently lost their thumbnail tile to an aspect-ratio edge case — invisible in loss, visible as a persistent doc-eval deficit on portrait scans. Found by binning eval errors by aspect ratio; that bin is now a standing report slice. When a capability underperforms its law, bin errors by every metadata field before touching the mixture.

Fitting-room truths

  • Loss spikes get shard-level autopsies. Top-3 causes: near-blank PDF pages with dense OCR labels, mega-resolution panoramas exploding tile count, corrupted JPEGs decoding to noise. The "spike gallery" slide deck of offending images built more stakeholder trust than any R².
  • Fits that died in the notebook (~5 per shipped fit): exponents pinned at bounds, CIs spanning sign flips, an α owned by two runs. Discipline: every coefficient ships with its bootstrap histogram attached, vetoable on the histogram alone.

Data-engine truths

  • ASR artifacts became model tics: speech-first captions carried "um", false starts, spoken numbers ("twenty twenty-three") — the round-1 captioner learned them; downstream models occasionally described images in spoken register. The normalization pass mattered as much as the protocol. "Human" is not the same as "clean."
  • Honeypots for annotators: as the captioner improved, human editors drifted to rubber-stamping. Seeded known-flawed captions at ~3%; honeypot catch-rate gates the throughput bonus. Catch rates jumped 20 points the week people learned honeypots existed. Data quality is mechanism design before it's ML.
  • The judge prompt is a hyperparameter with a changelog: one rubric line-edit shifted the T1/T2 split by 8% of the corpus. Judge prompts are version-controlled; tier IDs record the judge version that made them.

Organizational truths

  • The graph that convinced leadership was predicted-vs-actual with CI bands, next to the same panel for the previous vibes-era recipe — which was empty, because no prediction had existed. The empty panel did the persuading.
  • Sweeps are political currency: a block survives budget review if it's tied to a decision ("chooses recipe A vs B for the frontier run"), not a curiosity ("characterizes repetition decay"). Same experiments, different framing, different survival rate.
  • Deprecation is a data decision: deleting the round-1 caption corpus (50M captions, real money) was organizationally radioactive until reframed as a reversible iso-token experiment; the paired delta made the call; we archived. Frame destructive decisions as experiments and they become makeable.
Usage: one trench detail per answer, never more. The four to weave in unprompted: the realized-token audit, the bootstrap-histogram veto, the honeypot mechanism, decision-tied sweep blocks.

IV.4 · Three stories to tell (60–90s each, arc: mystery → clue → mechanism → rule)

Story 1 · The launch-date residual (how we caught an invisible experiment-killer)

"Halfway through the sweep, the mixing-law fit started degrading — new runs disagreed with old runs at the same mixture weights. Everyone's first theory was infra: bad nodes, a compiler change, a flaky seed. I did the boring thing and plotted residuals against every covariate we logged, including one nobody thinks of as a covariate: the date the run launched. Clean step function. Runs after a certain Tuesday sat on a different surface. That Tuesday, a partner team had shipped an improved OCR quality filter — better data, same source name, no announcement, because why would you announce an improvement? Twenty-five of my sixty sweep points were now trained on a distribution that no longer existed. We ate the loss, refit on post-change runs, and then made the structural fix: every source is referenced by an immutable snapshot ID plus filter version, and the fitter refuses any run whose IDs don't resolve. The rule I took away: in a data-science project, the data pipeline is part of the experiment, and anything not pinned is a confounder waiting for a Tuesday."

Story 2 · The judge that loved its sibling (measurement corrupting itself)

"CapForge's captioner improved every round — at least, the acceptance rate from our quality judge said so, climbing round over round. The team was celebrating. Something felt too smooth to me, so we ran a blinded human audit: humans rated round-2 and round-3 captions with no idea which was which. Verdict: flat. No improvement. The judge and the captioner descended from the same base model, and with each round the judge was increasingly rewarding its sibling's style — fluent, confident, template-shaped — rather than its accuracy. Our measurement instrument was slowly becoming a mirror. The fix had three parts: re-anchor the judge every round on human edit distance rather than its own score; build adversarial probes — captions with planted errors, a wrong count, an invented object — and gate every judge release on planted-error recall; and triangulate with an OCR-consistency check so no single scorer decides a tier. Acceptance rates stopped climbing, which was the correct outcome — the climb had been the artifact. The rule: any judge that shares lineage with what it judges will drift toward self-preference, and you will not notice, because the drift looks like progress."

Story 3 · The optimizer that walked off the map (model-based optimization's default failure)

"Our fitted mixing law had an R² of 0.93 on held-out mixtures, so we solved for the optimal weights — and the solution made me uneasy before I could say why: it maxed out three families simultaneously, sitting hard on the boundary of the feasible region. Then it clicked: our sixty Latin-hypercube proxy runs covered the interior of the simplex, and the optimizer had done exactly what optimizers do — walked to the region where the model was least constrained by data and most free to be optimistic. The fit wasn't wrong anywhere we'd measured; it was confidently extrapolating precisely where we hadn't. We re-allocated fifteen runs to the neighborhood of the proposed optimum — trust-region style — refit, and watched the 'optimal' point move by thirty percent relative in two families. We also changed the objective permanently: maximize predicted score minus a multiple of its own bootstrap uncertainty, so the solver is charged for entering territory the data can't defend. The rule generalizes way beyond data mixtures: the argmax of a fitted model lives in the model's blind spot by default — budget the second sampling round before you trust the first solve."

N1 · Context-length rung

Hyp: long-context MM data has its own exponent; 8K proxies structurally under-value it (the exact cause of both frontier misses). Exp: 32K rung at 1B via context extension on existing trunks; rerun 12 probe mixtures; fit L(N,D,w,ctx). ~15 runs. Kill: 32K ranking matches 8K within CI. Payoff: the first defended number for long-video share — currently the least-defended number in every MM recipe.

N2 · Resolution law (one currency for pixels & text)

Hyp: marginal value of the k-th image token saturates per task family (OCR late, natural scenes early) ⇒ content-conditional tiling beats any fixed policy at iso-FLOP. Exp: {64,256,576} tok/tile × 4 family-enriched mixes at 1B, image tokens displacing text tokens (the real tradeoff). Kill: saturation points within 2× across families ⇒ ship one global number. Payoff: "this image's 5th tile is worth 300 caption tokens" — the allocation primitive MM data science lacks.

N3 · Law-guided online mixing

Hyp: since w* moves with compute, the optimal schedule is the path w*(C(t)) — the fitted surface already contains the curriculum. Exp: 3 arms iso-FLOP at 1B: static w*(C_final) / law-implied path / DoReMi-style online controller; instrument for path-dependence the (path-blind) law can't see. Kill: path arm must beat static by >2σ with no guardrail regression. Payoff: mixture, curriculum, and anneal unify into one object — the anneal becomes the tail of the w*(C) path.

Mid-term bets

M1 · Scaling laws for the data engine itself

Frontier spend is shifting from crawling to manufacturing; nobody has published capability = f(C_gen, C_verify, H_seed). CapForge already fit three fragments (captioner cost knee, verification value curve, q vs human-edit rounds); the proposal stitches them with a designed grid. Prior to stake: verification compute is the under-bought term. Named risk: the surface refits every model generation — budget for it, don't pretend permanence.

M2 · Honest post-training laws

RL moves policies, not likelihoods — loss stops being the state variable. Use pass-rate-vs-compute on verifiable MM tasks; 3-axis grid of PT-gold dose (reusing T2 checkpoints as free independent variables — ~10× cost saving) × SFT dose × RL compute. Target: the elicitation frontier — iso-capability curves in the (PT gold tokens, RL compute) plane; its slope is the exchange rate the PT↔post debate has always lacked. Prediction: L-shaped for perception-bound capabilities, shallow for format-bound (B-P3's split, promoted to a law). Kill: pass-rate instability across seeds → fall back to best-of-k probes.

M3 · Transfer-matrix laws

Does cross-modal transfer grow with scale (models becoming modality-agnostic learners — the optimistic reading of early-fusion results)? Estimate the family×stream matrix Γ at 1B and 3B; test ‖Γ‖ growth; ~30 add-one-family runs. Load-bearing either way: transfer grows ⇒ data becomes fungible, buy the cheapest capable tokens; doesn't ⇒ per-modality supply is a permanent strategic constraint and the data engine (M1) is existential. Either answer reallocates real money — the definition of a well-posed bet.

Horizon — laws in representation space

Token loss over-rewards surface redundancy and under-measures perceptual grounding — it's a proxy state variable. The next instrument: measure data value by latent predictive quality — loss predicting frozen SSL/JEPA embeddings of masked/future visual content (the LatentFusion setting). Concrete two-week pilot, zero new training: re-evaluate the ~160 existing checkpoints on (a) token loss and (b) latent-prediction loss vs frozen DINOv2/V-JEPA targets; fit the same law family on both; the test is which better predicts the frontier eval vector we already measured. If (b) wins — my bet, because it's denoised of surface entropy — the follow-up optimizes mixtures directly against latent-prediction loss, and "what is a video token worth" finally gets a principled answer. The through-line: today's laws are built on a proxy metric; the next generation should be built on representations.

The 30-second vision answer: "Three steps. Near-term: make context length and image-token budget explicit law axes, and let the fitted surface drive the curriculum online. Mid-term: two missing laws — one pricing data-manufacturing compute against training compute, because that's where frontier spend is moving, and one honest post-training law built on pass-rate curves. Long-term: token loss is the wrong state variable for multimodal data science — I want laws fit in representation space, predicting frozen SSL embeddings, which connects to my own latent-prediction proposal. Better state variable, better science."

"Project A priced the constraint, so I started CapForge to relieve it: a speech-first human dense-caption seed, an iteratively human-refined captioner, quality tiers. Two pivots mattered. V.3 · Cross-exam Q&A (the full bank)

"How do you know your proxy predicts the frontier?" ▶

Gate on rank correlation to a held-out larger reference before trusting any fit (ρ≥0.8, per-eval); fit jointly over N so the optimum is extrapolated, not assumed; the frontier run is a verification of a prediction, and prediction residuals are the pipeline's primary quality metric. The one-time 8B reference is capital, amortized over the whole project — and it's not circular: it validates ranking fidelity of cheap runs, it doesn't set weights.

"Why exponential mixing laws instead of a neural predictor?" ▶

Sample efficiency and extrapolation honesty. With ~60 runs, a low-parameter form with the right inductive bias (diminishing returns per family) fits tightly and fails legibly. A neural predictor with 60 points memorizes the interior and extrapolates confidently wrong exactly where the optimizer goes. With 500+ runs I'd revisit — CLIMB-style predictor-guided iterative search is the upgrade path.

"Your mixture law has ~100 parameters. Overfit much?" ▶

Group-lasso killed most cross-terms (interference is sparse — an empirical finding); every headline claim validated on held-out mixtures by extrapolation and decision-rank, never fit quality; bootstrap-over-runs CIs on every coefficient, and where the CI was wide (video) we said so and declined to allocate. The parameter count is honest because the validation is out-of-sample.

"Why not just trust Chinchilla's exponents?" ▶

Exponents are distribution- and stack-dependent (tokenizer, context, interleaving, quality — and E certainly differs per stream). And replicating the base law calibrates my instrument: noise floor and extrapolation error, which set the CIs on everything downstream. You replicate not to learn the answer but to certify the instrument.

"Encoder frozen — why does encoder data still matter?" ▶

The encoder sets the perceptual ceiling: information it discards (fine text, small objects) can't be recovered by any downstream data. Encoder data science — resolution schedule, re-captioning quality, contrastive+SSL fusion — is upstream of everything the LLM can learn: highest leverage, least reversible.

"Where would you install chart reasoning: PT, anneal, SFT, or RL?" ▶

Test all four on cost-per-capability, but my prior: PT+anneal to build the latent perceptual-numeric substrate broadly and cheaply, then SFT/RLVR to elicit and format it with programmatically-verifiable rendered charts as the reward. Most post-training "gains" are elicitation of PT-latent skill — never try to install raw perception in SFT. And per our probes: perception-bound gaps are L-shaped in RL compute; no amount of RL rescues missing perception.

"Synthetic data helped the target eval — why not more?" ▶

Target lift can mask diversity collapse and OOD decay. Watch embedding coverage and OOD holdouts; cap the synthetic fraction as a function of base-model quality; keep synthetic as rare-skill filler behind a verifier — and audit the verifier itself (planted-error recall), because every synthetic pipeline is secretly a distillation of its judge.

"One number to allocate the whole budget?" ▶

No honest scalar exists — capabilities have different exponents and saturation points. The closest: marginal predicted-eval gain per FLOP per intervention (family, tier, stage), allocated where the derivative is steepest under interference and collapse guardrails. Allocation is constrained gradient-following on fitted laws, not a ratio.

"How does this change for post-training / RL data?" ▶

Two assumptions break: loss stops being the state variable (RL moves policies — use pass-rate curves on verifiable tasks), and value becomes order-dependent (curriculum effects dominate at post-training scale, violating the mixture-weight abstraction). Honest answer: post-training scaling laws are open; start from dose–response and paired ablations, and be suspicious of anyone selling smooth RLHF-data laws today.

"Single most consequential technical choice?" ▶

Per-stream validation losses instead of a scalar MM loss. It made E identifiable per modality, interference measurable as sparse cross-terms, loss→eval links stable, and the compute-dependent-mixture headline visible at all. Averaging is where multimodal signal goes to die.

"If you could keep only one result?" ▶

The effective-token discount + binding-constraint analysis. It converts "more data is better" into an allocation theory: when to stop buying volume and start buying uniqueness; it priced a data engine before we built it; and it bridges scaling-law thinking to data-engine investment — the core open question of the field right now.
