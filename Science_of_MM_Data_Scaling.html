§1 · What "science of data" actually means

"Science of data" is the discipline of turning data decisions from folklore ("more is better, dedup helps") into predictable, measured levers with laws attached. The frontier framing has four commitments:

1. Data is a design space, not a pile

Every corpus has axes — quantity, quality, diversity, freshness, modality mix, difficulty, provenance. Science means treating each as a knob you can turn and measure, not a fixed asset.

2. Predictable before large

The goal is a function: given a data recipe, predict downstream loss/eval at the target scale from small-scale runs. If you can't predict it, you don't understand it.

3. Marginal value, not average value

The question is never "is captions data good" — it's "what is the marginal eval gain per token of captions data given everything else already in the mix." Data value is contextual and saturating.

4. Compute-optimal allocation

Under a fixed FLOP/token budget, science tells you how to split the budget across modalities, quality tiers, and PT-vs-post — the multimodal generalization of Chinchilla.

The one-sentence thesis. The science-of-data job is to convert the token budget into the maximum capability per FLOP by discovering the mixture, curriculum, and quality function that generalize from proxy scale to frontier scale — and to express the answer as laws, not vibes.

Why the taxonomy matters for science. Each family has a different scaling exponent and a different saturation point. Averaging them ("we have 10B pairs") destroys the signal. The unit of study is the family × quality-tier cell, not the corpus.

§3 · Vision-encoder data science

The encoder (or the vision path of a native-multimodal model) determines the ceiling of perception; no amount of LLM post-training recovers detail the encoder discarded. Data questions here are distinct from the LLM's.

3.1 The contrastive-vs-generative data axis

  • Contrastive (CLIP/SigLIP-style) needs massive, diverse image–text pairs; benefits from scale and aggressive filtering (CLIP-score, dedup). Learns aligned but sometimes bag-of-words semantics.
  • Caption/generative pretraining (CapPa, generative encoders) needs denser, more grammatical text; teaches attributes and relations that contrastive misses.
  • Self-supervised (DINO/MAE/JEPA) needs no text at all — pure images; gives spatially rich features. The frontier move is fusing SSL + contrastive targets (relevant to my own LatentFusion line).

3.2 Resolution & tokens as a data axis

Native/any-resolution encoding turns pixels into a data-budget decision: a 4-tile 896px doc image costs ~4× the tokens of a thumbnail. "Science of data" for the encoder includes the resolution schedule — how many high-res tokens to spend on which content — because that's where DocVQA/ChartQA gains live.

Encoder data laws worth owning: (1) contrastive eval improves as a power law in unique pairs, not raw pairs — dedup shifts the curve left; (2) re-captioning web data with a strong VLM is one of the highest-ROI data interventions known (fixes the alt-text quality floor); (3) resolution has its own compute-optimal frontier per task family.

§4 · Pretraining-data science for the native-MM model

PT

4.1 The mixture problem (the central object)

A native-multimodal PT run draws from dozens of sources. The mixture weights — the sampling probability of each source — are the single most consequential data decision. The science:

Given sources S₁..Sₙ with weights w₁..wₙ (Σw=1),
find w* = argmax_w  Σ_tasks  λ_task · Eval_task( model trained on mix(w) )
subject to fixed token budget T and per-source availability caps.

This is intractable to sweep directly at scale, so science replaces the sweep with predictors: proxy-model regressions, data-mixing laws, and gradient/influence-based attribution (§5).

4.2 Modality balance & interference

Multimodal PT has a phenomenon single-modal doesn't: cross-modal interference / catastrophic forgetting of text. Push image tokens too high and the model's pure-text reasoning regresses; too low and perception is weak. The mixture must be tuned as a Pareto front (text quality ↔ vision quality), not a single optimum.

4.3 Curriculum & staging

  • Warmup on text → establish language backbone.
  • Ramp multimodal → introduce pairs, then interleaved, then video/audio.
  • Quality annealing / "mid-training" → in the final fraction of tokens, up-weight the cleanest, highest-density, hardest data (the biggest lever discovered in the last two years). Analogous to LR decay but for data quality.

4.4 Quality, dedup, and decontamination

Three under-glamorous but decisive pipelines: (1) quality classifiers per modality (aesthetic, caption-fidelity, OCR-density, toxicity); (2) near-dup detection across and within modalities (image hashing + text minhash); (3) eval decontamination — removing test images/captions that leak into training, which is harder in MM because the same image appears re-captioned, cropped, or re-encoded.

Brainstorm edge cases worth flagging in interview: the same figure appearing in a paper's PDF, its arXiv HTML, a blog re-post, and a screenshot — four "different" samples that are one concept (dedup + contamination nightmare). Or a benchmark image that a re-captioning model has described in training data (silent leakage).

§5 · Scaling laws & predictive methodology

5.1 Data-mixing laws

The key enabling result: downstream loss is a predictable, low-parameter function of mixture weights. A workable functional form:

L_task(w) ≈ c_task + Σ_i  a_{task,i} · exp( -b_{task,i} · w_i )     (mixing law)
# fit a,b,c from a handful of small proxy runs at varied w;
# then optimize w* analytically / with a tiny solver at target scale.

This converts an n-dimensional sweep into ~O(n) small runs + a regression — the workhorse of practical data science.

5.2 Joint data–compute laws (the MM Chinchilla)

L(N, D_text, D_img, D_vid, ...) — loss as a function of params and
per-modality token counts. Each modality contributes a term with its
own exponent; interference shows up as cross terms.
Compute-optimal: allocate marginal FLOP where ∂L/∂(FLOP) is steepest.

5.3 Attribution methods (which data caused which capability)

  • Leave-one-out / add-one-in proxy runs — gold standard, expensive.
  • Influence functions / TRAK-style estimators — approximate per-example influence on a target eval.
  • Gradient-alignment (DoGE/DoReMi-style) — reweight sources by how well their gradients align with a target distribution.
  • Datamodels — regress eval on the presence/absence of data groups.
The central risk of the whole enterprise: rank inversion across scale — a mixture that wins at the 1B proxy loses at 100B. Proxy design (making small runs predictive of large ones) is the hardest, most valuable skill.

§6 · Post-training-data science

Post

6.1 SFT: diversity > quantity, and the skill-coverage matrix

Post-training data science flips the PT intuition: past a few hundred K examples, adding more of the same helps little; what matters is coverage of the skill × modality × format matrix (counting, OCR, charts, spatial, multi-image, refusal, tool use...). Science = measuring the coverage grid and filling holes.

6.2 Synthetic data engines & the collapse boundary

  • Generation: use the current best model + tools (renderers, code, retrieval) to manufacture QA over images, especially for rare skills (chart reasoning, needle-in-video).
  • Verification: the value is in the filter, not the generator — verifiable tasks (code-rendered charts with known answers) let you keep only correct samples.
  • Collapse risk: naive self-training narrows the distribution. Science = measuring diversity (embedding coverage, n-gram/entropy) and capping the synthetic fraction.

6.3 RL/preference data

For VLLMs the crown-jewel problem is hallucination — describing objects not in the image. Preference/RLAIF data must be built to reward image-grounded honesty. Verifiable-reward RL (RLVR) extends to MM via programmatically-checkable visual tasks (counting, OCR exact-match, geometry).

6.4 The PT ↔ Post budget split

A genuinely open allocation question: a capability like chart reasoning can be bought with PT chart data, mid-training annealing, SFT, or RL. Each has a different cost curve and a different generalization footprint. Science-of-data means knowing which stage is the cheapest place to install a given capability — and post-training capabilities are often elicitation of what PT already latent-learned, not new learning.

§7 · Measurement & infrastructure (the unglamorous half)

Eval that isn't noise

Data experiments live or die on eval variance. You need low-variance, decontaminated, capability-decomposed suites — not a single leaderboard number. Confidence intervals on every claim.

Provenance & lineage

Every token traceable to source, license, filter version. Without lineage you can't do attribution or reproduce a mixture. This is a data-engineering problem masquerading as a research one.

Proxy-model infra

A cheap, fast, high-throughput proxy training harness is the lab bench. The whole science is bottlenecked by how many proxy runs/day you can turn.

Counterfactual bookkeeping

Every mixture decision should be an experiment with a control. Culture matters: "we added data X and eval went up" is not evidence unless X was the only change.

§8 · Open problems (brainstorm, think openly)

  1. Predictive proxies for emergent MM skills — losses predict smoothly; capabilities (OCR, counting) sometimes emerge sharply. Can we find proxy metrics that anticipate the jump?
  2. Cross-modal transfer laws — how much does text math data improve chart reasoning? Positive transfer is real but unquantified.
  3. The video token wall — video is where tokens explode; the science of which frames/patches are worth their tokens is wide open.
  4. Synthetic-real optimal ratio as a function of scale — does the safe synthetic fraction shrink or grow as the base model improves?
  5. Data as continuous, not batch — freshness/recency laws for a model that must know this month's world.
  6. Unified value metric — a single "nats of capability per token" currency across modalities that actually predicts allocation.

§9 · Deep insights — the hard-won truths beneath the taxonomy

Everything above is the map. These are the truths that only fall out of actually running the experiments — the layer that separates "has read the papers" from "has burned the compute." Each is stated as a claim you could defend, with its mechanism.

9.1 Loss is not capability, and the gap is structured

Stream loss improves smoothly; capabilities ride on top of it through link functions that are mostly sigmoidal — so "emergence" is usually a sharp link over smooth loss, not magic. The deep part: different capabilities sit at different points on their sigmoids at any given scale, which means the same data intervention looks transformative for one eval and useless for another even when it moved both losses equally. Naive readers conclude "data X helps counting but not OCR"; the truth may be "both moved 0.01 nats, but counting was mid-sigmoid and OCR pre-threshold." Misreading link position as data value is possibly the most common analytic error in MM data work.

9.2 Data value is a derivative, and derivatives don't commute

The marginal value of source X depends on everything already in the mix — formally, ∂L/∂w_x evaluated at the current w. Consequence that surprises people: two teams can run honest, well-controlled experiments on the same source and get opposite signs, because they evaluated the derivative at different base mixtures. "Is chart data good?" has no answer; "is chart data good at our mixture" does. This is also why published ablations transfer so poorly between labs — they're derivatives at undisclosed operating points.

9.3 The best data is the data the model almost understands

Quality classifiers rank data by human notions of cleanliness, but training value peaks at the edge of the model's current competence — too easy is redundant (gradient ≈ 0 information), too hard is noise (gradient ≈ interference). This is why q is N-dependent (bigger models extract more from dense data), why curriculum falls out of the same law, and why "quality" filtering can go negative at scale (DataComp-VLM's result): the filter deletes the hard-but-learnable band along with the garbage. The right mental model is a moving competence frontier, not a fixed quality bar.

9.4 Interference is sparse, and that's a gift

"Vision hurts text" is folk wisdom; measurement says interference concentrates in a few identifiable source×stream pairs (usually: high-volume low-density data crowding out its own modality's dense data, and format-heavy data bleeding format into free generation). Sparsity means interference is fixable by surgery — cap one family — rather than by retreat ("lower all image weight"). Labs that believe the folk version leave capability on the table permanently.

9.5 Multimodal dedup is a semantic problem wearing an infrastructure costume

The same visual concept arrives as: original photo, crop, re-encode, screenshot-of-a-tweet-containing-it, PDF figure, re-captioned copy, and a model-generated description of it. Hash-level dedup catches the first three; nothing standard catches the last three — and the last three are exactly how eval contamination and value double-counting actually happen at frontier labs. The uncomfortable conclusion: your unique-token count U — the denominator of every scaling claim — is soft, and it's soft in the direction of flattering you.

9.6 The anneal works because of what it displaces, not just what it adds

The standard story is "high-quality data + fast LR decay = steep gains." The doer's addendum: the anneal window is zero-sum in a way the base mix isn't — every gold token in the window displaces a base-mix token at the moment of highest learning rate per token. Half of the measured "anneal effect" in careful ablations is the removal of noisy data from the decay window, not the addition of gold. Test that distinguishes them: anneal on filtered-base-mix (no gold) captures a surprising fraction of the lift. This changes what you buy: cleaning the tail of the schedule is cheaper than manufacturing gold.

9.7 Every synthetic pipeline is secretly a distillation of its judge

The generator proposes; the verifier disposes; so the surviving distribution is shaped by the judge's preferences, blind spots, and biases — the generator's are filtered out. Long-run, models trained on verified synthetic data inherit judge biases at compound interest (each generation of data engine re-selects for them). Practical corollary: invest in judge diversity and adversarial judge-evals with the same seriousness as generator quality, because the judge is the actual teacher.

9.8 Mixture weights are a lie unless you audit realized tokens

You set w as sampling probabilities over documents; the model experiences tokens. Documents have wildly different lengths per family (an interleaved doc is 100× an alt-text pair), packing splits images across sequence boundaries, and loss masking changes which tokens even produce gradients. Nominal w and realized token-share routinely differ by 2× in unaudited pipelines. Any law fit on nominal weights is fit on a fiction — this single accounting discipline (log realized, masked, per-family token counts from the actual dataloader) silently invalidates a large fraction of public mixture research.

9.9 The eval suite is part of the training recipe

Whatever you measure, the optimizer (human or mathematical) maximizes. A saturated eval in the objective is dead weight that adds noise; an unrepresented capability silently decays; a contaminated eval actively steers data selection toward more contamination. Frontier practice treats the eval suite as a versioned, headroom-weighted, decontaminated component of the recipe — and re-derives it each cycle, because last cycle's optimization saturated last cycle's suite. Data science and eval science are one discipline with two names.

9.10 The half-life of a data law is one model generation

Exponents, q multipliers, and transfer terms are all conditioned on the architecture, tokenizer, context length, and — for synthetic data — the generator model. Ship a new base model and the surface must be refit. The durable assets are never the coefficients; they are the instrument (proxy ladders, calibration gates, realized-token audits, decontamination pipelines) and the functional forms. Teams that treasure coefficients ossify; teams that treasure instruments compound. This is the correct answer to "what did the project really produce?"

If you can only carry five: loss≠capability (link functions, §9.1) · data value is a derivative at your operating point (§9.2) · quality is a moving competence frontier (§9.3) · audit realized tokens or your law is fiction (§9.8) · the durable asset is the instrument, not the coefficients (§9.10).

CASE STUDY — "Science of MM Data" @ Gemini

Framing note (honesty): The following is a constructed narrative written in first person as if I had led this project inside Gemini, for interview-prep rehearsal. It's designed to be technically faithful to how such a project would actually run and fail — not a report of real proprietary work.

C1 · Charter & framing

The ask. Leadership set a token budget for the next-gen native-multimodal model and wanted a defensible data recipe: mixture weights, quality tiers, resolution schedule, and PT-vs-post allocation — each backed by a prediction, not a hunch. My charter: "Make the multimodal data recipe predictable. Give us a law we can allocate against."

Scope I owned. The pretraining mixture across ~40 sources spanning 8 modalities/families, the mid-training quality-anneal recipe, and the handoff of PT-latent capabilities into post-training.

Success metric. Not "higher eval" — that's too easy to fake. It was: predict the frontier-run eval vector within CI from proxy-scale fits, and beat the incumbent hand-tuned recipe at iso-FLOP.

C2 · Methodology (the machine I built)

  1. Instrument the corpus. Built per-source lineage + per-modality quality scores (caption-fidelity via a VLM judge, OCR density, aesthetic, dup-cluster id). Unit of study = family × quality-tier cell (~120 cells), not raw sources.
  2. Proxy ladder. Three rungs: 300M, 1B, 3B params, each with a fixed short token budget, tuned so their eval rankings correlate with a held-out 8B reference — proxies chosen for rank fidelity, not absolute loss.
  3. Mixing-law fitting. Ran ~60 proxy runs with Latin-hypercube-sampled mixture weights; fit the per-eval exponential mixing law (§5.1) with cross-modal cross-terms.
  4. Constrained optimization. Solved for w* under availability caps and a text-regression guardrail (pure-text eval not allowed to drop > X).
  5. Anneal design. Separately fit the value of quality-annealing the final 15% of tokens; treated anneal composition as its own small law.
  6. Verify at scale. One frontier run on w*, compared prediction vs actual, fed residuals back to refine the functional form.
# the objective I actually optimized
maximize   Σ_task λ_task · Ŝ_task(w, anneal)          # predicted eval vector
subject to  Σ w = 1
            w_i ≤ cap_i                                # can't exceed what exists
            Ŝ_textreasoning(w) ≥ S_floor               # anti-interference guardrail
            synthetic_fraction ≤ ρ(scale)              # anti-collapse guardrail

C4 · Real problems I faced — and how I solved them

Problem 1 · Rank inversion: the proxy lied at scale ▶

Symptom. The mixture that topped the 1B proxy underperformed at 8B on reasoning-heavy MM evals. Classic small-model-prefers-easy-data trap: at 1B, capacity is the bottleneck so noisy high-volume web pairs "look" great; at 8B, capacity frees up and dense/hard data pays off.

Fix. (1) Stopped fitting on absolute proxy loss and fit on rank against the 8B reference (Phase 0 gate, ρ≥0.8). (2) Added params as an explicit axis — fit the mixing law jointly over (w, N) so I could extrapolate the optimum in N rather than assume it's fixed. (3) Weighted proxy evals toward capabilities known to be capacity-gated. Net: predicted optimum tracked the 8B optimum instead of the 1B one.

Problem 2 · Eval variance drowned the signal ▶

Symptom. Run-to-run eval noise on small proxies (±2–3 pts) was as large as the mixture effect I was trying to measure. Every conclusion was seed noise.

Fix. Built a low-variance eval harness: larger eval sets, multi-seed proxy runs with variance-reduction (common random seeds / shared data order across arms so the difference is low-variance even if each arm isn't), and reported effects as paired deltas with bootstrapped CIs. Also switched marginal-value claims to slopes across a dose sweep rather than single-point comparisons — a slope is far more robust than a two-point diff.

Problem 3 · Silent eval contamination through re-captioning ▶

Symptom. A suspiciously good DocVQA number. Root cause: our re-captioning model had, in its own training, seen benchmark images and its generated captions leaked benchmark answers into our "synthetic dense caption" source — contamination the text-only dedup never caught because the pixels matched a test image while the text was freshly generated.

Fix. Moved decontamination to image space: perceptual-hash + embedding-NN against all eval images, applied before re-captioning, plus cross-checking generated captions for verbatim answer strings. Instituted a rule: any synthetic source inherits the contamination set of its generator's training data. Rescored — the DocVQA gain shrank to its honest size.

Problem 4 · Cross-modal interference (text regressed) ▶

Symptom. The eval-maximizing MM mixture quietly cost ~1.5 pts on pure-text reasoning — unacceptable, and invisible if you only watch MM dashboards.

Fix. Made it a constrained optimization, not a max: added the text-reasoning floor as a hard constraint (see C2 objective). Discovered via the fitted cross-terms that most interference came from one high-volume low-density image family; capping it specifically (rather than lowering all image weight) recovered text with near-zero MM cost. Interference is usually a few bad sources, not "vision vs language" globally.

Problem 5 · The video token wall blew the budget ▶

Symptom. Video at native frame rate consumed a wildly disproportionate token share for modest eval gain — the marginal-value curve (Phase 1) was near-flat past a low dose.

Fix. Reframed video as a token-efficiency problem, not a volume one: measured eval-per-token under different frame-sampling / token-budget-per-clip policies and found the compute-optimal frontier was aggressive temporal subsampling + keyframe selection. Allocated the freed tokens back to high-ROI OCR/doc data. Lesson I'd give in interview: for expensive modalities, the right knob is tokens-per-sample, not number-of-samples.

Problem 6 · Synthetic data quietly collapsed diversity ▶

Symptom. Up-weighting model-generated QA improved target evals but degraded out-of-distribution generalization; embedding-coverage of the synthetic set was visibly narrower than real data.

Fix. Added a diversity guardrail to the optimizer (synthetic_fraction ≤ ρ(scale)), measured via embedding-space coverage + response entropy. Invested in the verifier not the generator (kept only programmatically-checkable-correct samples), and forced generator diversity via templated tool/renderer variation. Kept synthetic as a coverage-filler for rare skills, never a bulk source.

Problem 7 · Attribution disagreed with dose–response ▶

Symptom. Influence-function attribution flagged a source as high-value, but a clean leave-one-out proxy run showed near-zero effect. Which do you trust?

Fix. Treated LOO/add-in proxy runs as ground truth and influence estimates as a cheap prefilter only. Used influence to rank candidate sources, then confirmed the top-k with actual ablation runs before any recipe change. Documented that first-order influence is unreliable under the strong data-mixing nonlinearity we'd measured — a good cautionary story about not shipping recipe changes on approximate attribution alone.

Problem 8 · Stakeholders wanted "add my dataset" mid-flight ▶

Symptom. The organizational version of a data problem: teams lobbying to inject their favorite corpus, each anecdotally "great." Uncontrolled, this destroys the science.

Fix. Instituted an evidentiary bar: any new source enters via a standard Phase-1 dose–response + Phase-2 mix-in run; it earns weight only if its marginal value at the current mixture clears CI. Turned "who lobbies hardest" into "what does the marginal-value curve say." This process discipline was, honestly, half the actual value of the project.

C5 · Results, and the laws it left behind

  • Prediction held: frontier-run eval vector landed within CI of the proxy-fit prediction for the majority of the suite — the recipe was chosen, then confirmed, not stumbled into.
  • Beat the hand-tuned incumbent at iso-FLOP on the aggregate MM suite with no pure-text regression (the guardrail did its job).
  • Reusable artifacts: the mixing-law fitter, the family×tier corpus instrumentation, the image-space decontamination rule, and the evidentiary intake process outlived the specific model — that's the real deliverable of a "science of data" team.
  • Durable laws: per-family marginal-value curves, the quality-anneal value function, and a video tokens-per-sample compute-optimal frontier.
The meta-lesson I'd lead with in interview: the win wasn't a magic dataset. It was converting data decisions into measured, predicted, constrained optimization — and building the process so that every future recipe change had to clear a marginal-value bar. Science-of-data is 30% modeling, 30% eval/infra hygiene, and 40% making the org decide with evidence.

C6 · Interview Q&A rehearsal

"How would you know your proxy predicts the frontier?" ▶

I gate on rank correlation to a held-out larger reference before trusting any fit (Spearman ρ≥0.8), and I fit jointly over params so I extrapolate the optimum in N rather than assume the small-model optimum transfers. The frontier run is a verification of a prediction, and I track prediction residuals as the primary quality metric of the whole pipeline.

"Vision encoder is frozen — why does encoder data still matter?" ▶

Because the encoder sets the perceptual ceiling: information it discards (fine text, small objects, spatial precision) can't be recovered by any downstream data. So encoder data science — resolution schedule, re-captioning quality, contrastive+SSL fusion — is upstream of everything the LLM can learn. It's the highest-leverage, least-reversible data decision.

"Where would you install chart-reasoning: PT, anneal, SFT, or RL?" ▶

Empirically I'd test all four on a cost-per-capability basis, but my prior: PT + quality-anneal to build the latent perceptual+numeric substrate cheaply and broadly, then SFT/RLVR to elicit and format it, using programmatically-verifiable rendered charts so RL has a clean reward. Most post-training "gains" are elicitation of PT-latent skills — so I'd never try to install raw chart perception in SFT alone.

"Your synthetic data helped the target eval — why not use more?" ▶

Because target-eval lift can mask diversity collapse and OOD degradation. I watch embedding-coverage and OOD holdouts, cap the synthetic fraction as a function of base-model quality, and keep synthetic as a rare-skill coverage-filler behind a verifier — the verifier is where the value is, not the generator.

"One number to allocate the whole budget?" ▶

There isn't one honest scalar — capabilities have different exponents and saturation points. The closest is marginal predicted-eval gain per FLOP for each candidate intervention (family, tier, stage), allocating where that derivative is steepest under the interference and collapse guardrails. Allocation is a constrained gradient-following on the fitted laws, not a single ratio.
