Part B · Background§5 · How modern image generators actually work

The paper never discloses Nano Banana Pro's architecture, so you must be fluent in both families it could be — and in what they share: a learned mapping from (noise or tokens) + conditioning → pixels, trained at web scale.

5.1 Diffusion (the likely substrate of most frontier editors)

Forward process: gradually noise a clean image $x_0$ over $T$ steps. The closed form at step $t$ (with schedule $\bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s\le t}(1-\beta_s)$):

$$ q(x_t \mid x_0) = \mathcal{N}\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0,\; (1-\bar\alpha_t)\,\mathbf{I}\big) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\varepsilon $$
The closed form, rendered: the same $x_0$ at five values of $\bar\alpha_t$. Training shows the network every point on this ramp and asks for the noise back — so at every level it must maintain a scene hypothesis (what's sky, what's object, what occludes what). That is the perception it later gets asked to output.

Training: a network $\varepsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c)$ (U-Net or, at frontier scale, a DiT — a transformer over latent patches) learns to predict the noise, conditioned on text $c$:

$$ \mathcal{L} = \mathbb{E}_{x_0,\varepsilon,t}\,\big\lVert \varepsilon - \varepsilon_\theta(x_t, t, c) \big\rVert^2 $$

Sampling: start from pure noise, iteratively denoise (dozens of steps — this is why one prediction costs "a full generation"). Classifier-free guidance sharpens conditioning by extrapolating between conditional and unconditional predictions:

$$ \hat\varepsilon = \varepsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing) + w\,\big(\varepsilon_\theta(x_t, c) - \varepsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing)\big), \qquad w > 1 $$
Why this matters for the talk The denoiser is trained to reconstruct plausible scenes from partial information at every noise level. That objective forces internal estimates of grouping, occlusion, depth ordering, lighting — the "generation strictly requires implicit perception" argument is a statement about what this loss demands. DIFT (2023) showed these internal features already do correspondence; Vision Banana is the industrial-strength version of the same observation.

5.2 Autoregressive image generation (the other possibility)

Quantize images into discrete tokens with a VQ-VAE/VQGAN codebook; train a transformer with plain next-token cross-entropy over interleaved text+image tokens; decode tokens back to pixels. Gemini-lineage image models are plausibly this (or hybrid). Two consequences worth citing in Q&A: (1) an AR generator exposes token logprobs — one of the uncertainty routes on the limits slide; (2) "a long AR decode over image tokens" is the AR version of the inference-cost argument.

Either substrate ends at the same place — an RGB image — which is exactly why re-parameterizing tasks as RGB makes the method architecture-agnostic.

Part B · Background§6 · The VAE and where the 8-bit ceiling really comes from

Latent generators don't emit pixels directly; a VAE decoder maps compressed latents (typically 8× spatial downsampling, 4–16 channels) back to RGB. Three consequences you cite in the talk:

  • Color drift: reconstruction is lossy — emitted colors deviate a few 8-bit units from targets. This is why the seg decoder needs tolerance $\tau=14$ and why "well-separated codewords" is a design rule, not pedantry.
  • Banding on smooth ramps: VAEs allocate capacity to texture and edges; a perfectly smooth grayscale gradient is reconstructed with visible steps. This is argument #2 for the rainbow path — false-color imagery with structure is closer to the training distribution than laboratory-smooth ramps.
  • The real ceiling is compound: 8-bit quantization and VAE reconstruction error and sampling stochasticity. The napkin calc shows the first is negligible; the honest statement is "generation fidelity — VAE + sampling — is the bottleneck."
Why the encoding avoids smooth ramps: an ideal gradient (top) comes back from a lossy reconstruction with visible steps (middle, quantization exaggerated to 12 levels to make the effect legible). The rainbow path (bottom) carries ~7× more levels and looks like the false-color imagery the generator was trained on.
Sharp follow-up you should expect "Couldn’t they decode in latent space and skip the VAE error?" — Yes, that’s precisely the latent-pole design (JEPA-style): predict in a representation space and read out with learned probes. The cost: you lose human-inspectability and the reuse of the generator's pixel-space prior. The trade is the pixel-vs-latent interface axis in miniature.

Part B · Background§7 · Instruction tuning, replay, and the superficial-alignment hypothesis

Instruction tuning = supervised finetuning on (instruction, response) pairs with the same loss as pretraining — for an image model, (image + instruction) → target answer-image. No new loss, no new heads; only the data changes.

Catastrophic forgetting & replay: finetuning on a narrow distribution drags all weights toward it. The standard mitigation — used verbatim here — is mixing the new data into the original mixture at a low ratio, keeping the gradient distribution near pretraining. That is why generation survives at 53.5%/47.8% win rates. Know the term replay and the LLM analogue (continued pretraining mixes).

LIMA / superficial alignment (2023): ~1k curated examples suffice to align a pretrained LLM — evidence that capability lives in pretraining and post-training mostly teaches format and style. Vision Banana's low mixture ratio + pseudo/synthetic-only labels + emergent compositions is the same signature. The falsifiable prediction: performance vs tuning-set size should saturate early (Marigold's 74k is the precedent) — which is exactly reviewer-ablation #2 in your talk.

Say the mechanism, not just the slogan Why is format-teaching gradient-cheap? Because the features needed to solve the task already exist; the gradients only need to wire outputs to them, and consistent low-variance gradients (same format every example) move few directions in weight space. A high mixture ratio would instead push global style toward flat-color visualization outputs — style drift, the failure the replay ratio is tuned to avoid.

Part B · Background§8 · Metrics cheat-sheet — every number's denominator

Segmentation

IoU per class: $\text{IoU} = \frac{|P\cap G|}{|P\cup G|} = \frac{TP}{TP+FP+FN}$. mIoU: mean over classes (Cityscapes: 19). Punishes both leaked and missed pixels.

cIoU (RefCOCOg): cumulative IoU over the dataset — $\frac{\sum_i |P_i\cap G_i|}{\sum_i |P_i\cup G_i|}$; large objects dominate. gIoU (ReasonSeg): mean per-image IoU; every image counts equally. Know which is which — it's a classic gotcha.

cgF1 (SA-Co): F1 gated on classification — you're scored on correctly returning nothing for absent queries. This is the metric that exposed the no-abstain weakness.

Depth & normals

δ₁: fraction of pixels with $\max\!\big(\tfrac{\hat d}{d}, \tfrac{d}{\hat d}\big) < 1.25$ — "within 25% relative error." Higher is better; 0.929 means 93% of pixels land inside the band.

AbsRel: $\frac{1}{N}\sum \frac{|\hat d - d|}{d}$ — mean relative error; the metric the power transform is designed around (constant relative resolution).

Metric vs affine-invariant: affine-invariant methods (Marigold, MiDaS) are evaluated after fitting per-image scale+shift to ground truth; metric means raw meters, no alignment. VB's depth is metric — a strictly harder claim.

Normals: mean angular error $= \operatorname{arccos}(\mathbf{n}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}})$ averaged over pixels, in degrees; ↓ better.

Worked: |G|=100, |P|=110, overlap 80 → IoU = 80/(80+30+20) = 0.62. mIoU averages this per class.

The band is multiplicative — exactly why the depth code spends resolution proportionally to $1/d$ and the power transform beats a linear one.

Metric traps in Q&A ① δ₁ and AbsRel are relative — that's the entire justification for spending code resolution on near depth. ② cIoU≈73.8 vs gIoU≈79.3 are not comparable numbers — different averaging. ③ cgF1's negative queries are why "needs an MLLM filter" is a metric-driven finding, not an implementation excuse.

Part B · Background§9 · Monocular metric depth — the geometry you must be able to derive

Pinhole projection: a 3-D point $(X, Y, Z)$ lands at pixel $u = f\,\frac{X}{Z} + c_x$ (focal length $f$ in pixels). The scale ambiguity: scale the whole scene by $s$ — $(sX, sY, sZ)$ — and scale $f$ by nothing… the projection changes. But scale the scene by $s$ and shoot with focal length $f$ from distance $sZ$: $u = f\,\frac{sX}{sZ} + c_x$ — identical image. So from one image, $\{$scene at 1×, camera A$\}$ and $\{$scene at 2×, camera B$\}$ are indistinguishable: absolute depth is unidentifiable without knowing the camera.

How the model escapes: it doesn't invert geometry — it does inference under a prior. Web photos concentrate on a narrow joint distribution of (focal length, scene type, object scale): people are ~1.7 m, doors ~2 m, cars ~4.5 m, horizons sit where ground planes say. Formally: the likelihood is scale-invariant, but the posterior $p(d\mid \text{image}) \propto p(\text{image}\mid d)\,p(d)$ concentrates because the prior does.

  • Predictable failures = prior violations: telephoto compression & macro (atypical $f$), scale-free textures (no known-size anchors), miniatures/tilt-shift (adversarial anchors), fisheye (non-pinhole).
  • The thesis link: tuning saw only synthetic renders — so the (camera, scene) prior was already in the generator from pretraining. Rehearse this causal chain; it is the intellectual peak of the talk.
  • Contrast to know: Depth Anything 3 / MoGe-2 train on enormous real depth mixtures and often use camera conditioning or per-dataset alignment; VB's "no intrinsics, no real depth, still metric" is the differentiator.

Self-test — 12 questions

Click to reveal. Same bar as the interview: precise, mechanistic, no hedging.

1 · Derive the depth quantization error at d = 10 m from scratch. ▶

$\Delta t = 1/(7\cdot255) \approx 5.6\times10^{-4}$. From $t=1-(1+d/10)^{-2}$: $dt/dd = 0.2(1+d/10)^{-3}$, so $\Delta d = \Delta t / (dt/dd) = \Delta t \cdot 5(1+d/10)^3$. At $d=10$: $5.6\times10^{-4}\cdot5\cdot8 = 2.2$ cm ⇒ 0.22% relative — ~30× below the model's ~8% AbsRel.

2 · Why exactly does the rainbow path beat grayscale? (Need all three.) ▶

(1) Precision: 7×255≈1785 levels vs 256 (+2.8 bits). (2) Distribution match: false-color scientific viz is in web pretraining; smooth ramps trigger VAE banding. (3) Noise geometry: decode projects 3-D noise onto a 1-D path — off-path components are discarded, and axis-aligned edges mean only one channel's noise moves you along the path (vs all three for the gray diagonal: advantage ≈ 7/√3 ≈ 4×).

3 · State the monocular scale ambiguity precisely, then explain how VB emits meters anyway. ▶

Pinhole: $u=fX/Z$. Scaling the scene by $s$ with focal length adjusted keeps the image identical, so depth is unidentifiable from the likelihood alone. VB does Bayesian inference: web (camera, scene) pairs are concentrated — known object sizes, ground planes, perspective statistics — so the posterior over metric depth concentrates. Failure cases (telephoto, macro, miniatures, scale-free textures, fisheye) are prior violations, predicted by the theory. Since tuning was synthetic-only, that prior came from generative pretraining.

4 · Why does a low mixture ratio + replay preserve generation? ▶

Replay keeps the sampled gradient distribution close to pretraining's, suppressing catastrophic forgetting. The vision data can be a tiny fraction because format-teaching is gradient-cheap: the needed features exist; consistent low-variance gradients only wire the output format. A high ratio would drag global style toward flat-color visualization outputs. Receipts: 53.5% T2I win rate (parity/slightly up), 47.8% editing.

5 · cIoU vs gIoU vs cgF1 — define all three and name each one's bias. ▶

cIoU (RefCOCOg): cumulative $\sum|P\cap G| / \sum|P\cup G|$ — biased toward large objects. gIoU (ReasonSeg): per-image IoU averaged — every image equal. cgF1 (SA-Co): F1 gated on presence classification — scores abstention on negative queries, the exact capability pixel-space output lacks (hence the Gemini pre-filter).

6 · What claim does Vision Banana NOT establish, and what's the cheapest experiment that would? ▶

Two: (1) that pixels-only generative pretraining suffices (NBP's mixture is undisclosed, Gemini lineage likely includes understanding data) — test with pretraining-attribution ablations; (2) that the generalist wins per FLOP (no FLOP-matched specialist) — an intercept win, not a slope win. The base-probe has been run: untuned NBP falls far short, so prompting alone can't read the capability out — that makes the tuning-set-size scaling curve the decisive remaining test (early saturation ⇒ cheap format lesson; continued climbing ⇒ tuning builds capability), plus pretraining-attribution for claim (1) and the FLOP-matched specialist for claim (2).

7 · Walk the five instance-decoder stages with constants, and give one failure mode for the pipeline. ▶

① Background removal at tolerance τ=14. ② Seeded floodfill, 16-connectivity, threshold τ². ③ Prune components <0.02% area. ④ 3×3 binary erosion, θ=0.1. ⑤ Merge same-colored components whose bboxes expanded γ=5× overlap. Failure modes: near-identical colors on two instances merge them; erosion shrinks masks; γ-merge fuses distant same-colored instances; VAE drift consumes the τ budget.

8 · Why measure generation retention with win rates rather than FID? ▶

FID compares aggregate feature statistics and smooths over distribution-level style drift; a side-by-side win rate against the model's own base directly detects "did finetuning change outputs humans can notice," per prompt. For a claim about retention (no drift), the paired comparison is the right instrument.

9 · Where does the everything-as-RGB interface structurally fail? ▶

(1) No null output — can't abstain, hallucinated masks on negative queries. (2) No logits — no native confidence/calibration; ensembles cost K× generations. (3) Inference cost — a full generation per prediction. (4) Sparse structured outputs (boxes, keypoints) waste it; video tracking needs cross-frame color persistence that isn't guaranteed. Rule: dense fields → pixels; sparse structure → tokens.

10 · What did Marigold prove, and what exactly did Vision Banana add over it? ▶

Marigold: 74k synthetic pairs turn SD-2 into zero-shot SOTA affine-invariant depth — the prior transfers from tiny data, but per-task and scale-ambiguous. VB adds: frontier-scale base, metric depth without intrinsics, many tasks in one checkpoint, language task-spec (open vocab), and generation retained rather than sacrificed.

11 · Your proposed experiment — state it as a plot with axes. ▶

The scaling law of emergent perception. X: generator pretraining compute (FLOPs). Y: perception readout quality after a fixed small format-tuning budget (e.g., δ₁, mIoU). Second curve: tuning-data needed to reach a quality threshold vs compute — superficial alignment predicts it shrinks. Companion: 4-arm FLOP-matched grid (generalist vs specialist × pixel vs latent readout) to settle the per-FLOP and interface questions simultaneously.

12 · Sixty-second whiteboard: sketch the full system and label where task specificity lives. ▶

[image + instruction] → [frozen-architecture generator, instruction-tuned] → [RGB answer image] → [deterministic decoder] → [masks/depth/normals]. Task specificity lives in exactly two places: the prompt (input side: class-color mappings, colormap choice, target description) and the decode rules (output side: nearest-color, path projection + inverse transform, unit-normalization). The weights in the middle are task-agnostic — that's the whole design.
