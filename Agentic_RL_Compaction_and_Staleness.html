Agentic RL, formalized

Per-call trajectories, why compaction breaks the flat-sequence objective, and how asynchronous staleness is corrected. Companion to Post_Training_and_RL_Deep_Dive §2–§3.

1  Setup: the per-call decomposition

An agentic episode is a sequence of $K$ LLM calls. At call $k$ the model reads a context $c_k$ and generates a block of tokens $y_k = (y_{k,1},\dots,y_{k,T_k})$ (thought + tool call); the environment then executes and returns an observation $o_k$ (tool output). Only the $y_k$ are actions; the $o_k$ are dynamics.

$$ \tau \;=\; \big(c_1,\, y_1,\, o_1,\; c_2,\, y_2,\, o_2,\;\dots,\; c_K,\, y_K\big), \qquad R(\tau) \in \{0,1\} \text{ at the end (task success)} $$

The policy's total log-probability — the only thing the gradient ever touches — is a sum over policy-generated tokens only, each conditioned on the context actually seen at generation time:

$$ \log \pi_\theta(\tau) \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{K} \sum_{t=1}^{T_k} \log \pi_\theta\!\big(y_{k,t}\,\big|\; c_k \oplus y_{k,\lt t}\big) \tag{1} $$

where $\oplus$ is concatenation. Tool outputs $o_k$ contribute no terms — exactly as the environment dynamics $p(s'|s,a)$ cancelled out of $\nabla_\theta \log P(\tau|\theta)$ in the REINFORCE derivation. Masking is that cancellation, implemented.

When flattening is legal

Implementations like to flatten the episode into one long token sequence with a trainability mask. Equation (1) is equivalent to the flat form if and only if contexts are append-only:

$$ c_{k+1} \;=\; c_k \oplus y_k \oplus o_k \qquad \text{(prefix property)} \tag{2} $$

Then every conditioning context is a literal prefix of the flat sequence, one forward pass scores the whole episode, and the mask selects which positions contribute loss terms. All standard multi-turn RL training relies on (2).

2  Compaction breaks the prefix property

With long horizons the scaffold applies a compaction operator $\varphi$ (summarize / truncate) somewhere mid-episode:

$$ c_{k+1} \;=\; \varphi\big(c_k \oplus y_k \oplus o_k\big), \qquad \varphi \;\neq\; \mathrm{id} \tag{3} $$

Figure 1. Top: append-only contexts make the episode one flat maskable sequence. Bottom: after $\varphi$, call 3's true conditioning context is the summary — replaying the raw history would score different (wrong) log-probs. The episode must be stored and scored as per-call (context, generation) pairs.

What stays true, what changes

The gradient stays valid. $\varphi$ contains no $\theta$, so it is part of the environment transition. Writing the trajectory density with compaction,

$$ P(\tau|\theta) \;=\; \prod_{k=1}^{K}\;\underbrace{\pi_\theta(y_k \mid c_k)}_{\text{policy}}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{p(o_k \mid \text{history})\; \mathbb{1}\!\big[c_{k+1} = \varphi(c_k \oplus y_k \oplus o_k)\big]}_{\text{dynamics: no }\theta\ \Rightarrow\ \text{vanishes from } \nabla_\theta \log P} \tag{4} $$

so the policy-gradient estimator is unchanged in form: $\displaystyle \nabla_\theta J = \mathbb{E}\Big[\hat A \cdot \sum_k \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_k|c_k)\Big]$ — with $c_k$ the compacted context. Model-free means compaction-free gradients.

Intuition: the ledger audit

The $\log$ turns the trajectory product (4) into a ledger — one entry per event. $\nabla_\theta$ walks the ledger asking each entry one question: did $\theta$ have any say in this?

Ledger entryWho acted$\nabla_\theta$ contribution
$\log \pi_\theta(y_k \mid c_k)$the policy chosea gradient term — $\theta$ signed this line
$\log p(o_k \mid \cdot)$tool / sandbox / webzero — no $\theta$ inside
$\log \mathbb{1}[c_{k+1} = \varphi(\cdot)]$the scaffold's compactorzero — same summary for every $\theta$

Compaction is a clerk who replaces the case file with a summary mid-trial: it changes what happens next, but the clerk wasn't following $\theta$, so no change to $\theta$ changes what the clerk did — its derivative is zero by definition, not approximation. The policy is audited only on: given the (summarized) file you were handed, was your next move good? This is the same cancellation that makes REINFORCE model-free — tool outputs and compaction are both just "the world moved."

Valid gradient — of which objective? The estimator is exactly unbiased for $J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}[R]$ with $\varphi$ in the loop — the reward of the policy living with this compactor — not for the hypothetical full-memory objective. Three consequences: (i) the optimum of the compacted game can be worse than the full-context optimum; that gap is the POMDP cost and no gradient closes it — only a better $\varphi$ does; (ii) the policy can legitimately adapt to the compactor (e.g. learn to restate key facts so they survive summarization) — that optimization flows entirely through the $\pi_\theta$ ledger lines; (iii) if the policy writes its own summaries, those entries switch from "clerk" to "choice," acquire $\theta$, and become trainable actions (previous box).

But the problem becomes a POMDP. If $\varphi$ is lossy, $c_k$ no longer determines the true environment state $s_k$; the policy acts on an observation:

$$ \underbrace{s_k \xrightarrow{\;\varphi\;} c_k}_{\text{lossy observation}}, \qquad \pi_\theta(y_k \mid c_k) \;\neq\; \pi_\theta(y_k \mid s_k) $$

Two practical corollaries. (i) The critic also only sees $c_k$ — value estimates inherit the information loss, further weakening PPO's credit assignment on long horizons. (ii) No gradient can recover a fact $\varphi$ deleted; if performance is capped by compaction, the fix is $\varphi$, not the RL.

If the policy writes its own summaries, compaction becomes an action. Suppose the summary is generated by the policy: $c_{k+1} = y^{\text{sum}}_k \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid c_k \oplus y_k \oplus o_k)$. Then the summary tokens enter (1) as ordinary action tokens — unmasked, weighted by $\hat A$ — and the terminal reward trains what to remember. Same theorem, one more action block per compaction. (Learned-memory research; not standard practice yet.)

Intuition: the clerk becomes a defendant

In ledger terms, the compaction line flips category. It used to read $\mathbb{1}[c_{k+1}=\varphi(\cdot)]$ — deterministic, $\theta$-free, gradient zero. Now it reads $\pi_\theta(y^{\text{sum}}_k \mid \cdot)$ — a policy factor like any other, auditable by $\nabla_\theta$.

What the reward teaches — with no summarization labels anywhere. The supervision is indirect: episode succeeded → what you kept was sufficient → keep like that more often; episode failed → your actions were bad, or you forgot the thing that mattered — both get pushed down. Concretely: the agent notes a config path at call 12; compaction fires at call 15; the path is needed at call 25. Runs whose summaries kept the path succeed → positive $\hat A$ flows to those summary tokens; runs that dropped it flail → negative $\hat A$. Across episodes the gradient performs credit assignment over memory decisions — it discovers from task outcomes alone which facts are load-bearing.

The POMDP escape hatch. With fixed $\varphi$ we said: if $\varphi$ deletes the fact, no gradient can recover it. Learned compaction moves the observation function inside the action space — the agent partially controls what it will later get to observe. It is learning its own state representation: the RNN's "what to carry in the hidden state" question, at the scale of language and tools, with the memory as readable text instead of an opaque vector.

Why it isn't standard yet. (i) Ultra-long credit assignment: a summary affects reward only through decisions dozens of calls later; under GRPO broadcast (5) the memory-specific signal arrives only statistically over many episodes — very low SNR. (ii) Confounding: bad actions and bad memory are punished together; disentangling needs counterfactual groups (same prefix, different summaries) or a process reward on summaries — which reintroduces "what is a good summary" as a labeling problem. (iii) Degenerate memory: under pure outcome reward, summaries can drift toward self-serving reports (if a judge reads them) or dense private shorthand — performant but unauditable. (iv) Exploration: RL only reweights what the base model's summarization prior already samples; a memory strategy never tried is never reinforced.

Credit assignment on the per-call trajectory

With the episode as calls $k=1..K$, the two estimators differ only in the weight given to call $k$'s tokens:

$$ \text{GRPO:}\quad \hat A^{(i)}_{k,t} \;=\; \frac{R_i - \mathrm{mean}(R_{1..G})}{\mathrm{std}(R_{1..G})} \;\;\;\forall k,t \qquad \text{(one scalar per episode, broadcast)} \tag{5} $$ $$ \text{PPO+critic:}\quad \hat A_{k,t} \;=\; \sum_{l\ge 0} (\gamma\lambda)^l\, \delta_{k,t+l}, \qquad \delta_{k,t} = r_{k,t} + \gamma V_\phi(\cdot_{k,t+1}) - V_\phi(\cdot_{k,t}) \qquad \text{(per-token, needs a trainable } V_\phi) \tag{6} $$

(5) gives every token in every call the same credit — one bit of signal per episode, robust, critic-free. (6) localizes credit inside the episode but requires $V_\phi$ to predict success from mid-trajectory (compacted!) contexts. The trade is unresolved; turn-level baselines (e.g. grouping calls that share a state) are the active middle ground.

3  Asynchronous rollouts: staleness and its correction

Episodes take minutes–hours (tool latency), so rollout workers and the learner run concurrently. An episode begun under policy version $\theta_{v}$ finishes while the learner is at $\theta_{v+n}$ — the data is stale beyond what PPO's single $\theta_\text{old}$ snapshot assumes.

Figure 2. Asynchronous training. Long episodes straddle learner updates; the batch mixes data from several behavior-policy versions. Staleness is handled per token: log $\log\mu(y_{k,t}|\cdot)$ at generation time, importance-correct against the current policy, truncate the ratio, cap the allowed version gap.

The corrected objective

Let $\mu$ denote the behavior policy — the exact parameter version that generated a given token (logged at rollout time). The off-policy policy gradient re-weights each token by how the current policy differs from $\mu$:

$$ \nabla_\theta J \;\approx\; \mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\mu}\Big[\;\sum_{k,t}\; \underbrace{\min\!\Big(\frac{\pi_\theta(y_{k,t}\mid \cdot)}{\mu(y_{k,t}\mid \cdot)},\; \bar c\Big)}_{\text{truncated IS ratio}} \;\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(y_{k,t}\mid\cdot)\;\hat A_{k,t}\Big] \tag{7} $$

Three deliberate approximations, each trading bias for variance:

ChoiceExact alternativeWhy we don't
Per-token ratios, not the product over the trajectory$\prod_{k,t} \frac{\pi_\theta}{\mu}$ (true trajectory IS)a product of thousands of ratios explodes or vanishes — unusable variance; per-token is biased but bounded
Truncation at $\bar c$ (V-trace / truncated IS, $\bar c \approx 1$–$2$)untruncated ratio (unbiased)caps the variance of rare huge ratios; bias vanishes as $\pi_\theta \to \mu$
Staleness cap: drop data with version gap $> n_{\max}$keep everythingbeyond a gap, $\hat A$ computed under old $\mu$ is meaningless (the stale-advantage problem, magnified)

PPO's clipped objective is the special case where $\mu = \pi_{\theta_\text{old}}$ is a single recent snapshot and the truncation is the two-sided clip. Asynchronous agentic RL generalizes it: many $\mu$'s per batch (decoupled behavior/proximal policies, as in IMPALA/V-trace and recent decoupled-PPO variants), one-sided truncation, explicit staleness budget.

One sentence to retain: per-call segmentation makes compaction representable ((1) + (3)); the $\theta$-free-ness of $\varphi$ keeps the gradient valid (4); lossiness makes it a POMDP (fix $\varphi$, not the loss); and asynchrony is handled by logging the behavior policy per token and applying truncated importance ratios under a staleness cap (7) — the CPI/trust-region story, stretched across time.
