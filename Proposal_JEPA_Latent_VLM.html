LatentFusion: Multimodal Language Models that Predict in Frozen Self-Supervised Latent Spaces

Research Proposal · July 2026 · draft v0.2

Abstract

Current unified multimodal models couple language modeling with pixel-level generative objectives — discrete VQ tokens (Chameleon) or diffusion over image latents (Transfusion). Both spend capacity on high-frequency visual detail irrelevant for reasoning, and neither exploits the strong predictive representations learned by self-supervised vision models. We propose LatentFusion: a language model that consumes images through a standard text-aligned vision tower (CLIP/CoCa) but is trained to predict upcoming visual content in frozen self-supervised latent spaces — DINOv2 for image spans (dense semantics, geometry) and video-JEPA for video spans (dynamics, physics). Input and target spaces are deliberately decoupled: the model sees in a text-aligned space and predicts in spaces encoding exactly what text alignment lacks. Targets are frozen and precomputed, so collapse is impossible by construction. We study four objectives — dense regression, contrastive (InfoNCE), discretized-latent cross-entropy, and a lightweight latent flow head — as progressively stronger treatments of regression-to-the-mean. Hypothesis: stronger spatial, temporal, and world-model capability per FLOP than pixel-generative training, trading away native synthesis (recoverable via a separately trained decoder).

One-line pitch: a language model that sees in CLIP space and learns to predict in DINO/JEPA space acquires the dense-spatial and temporal world knowledge that text-aligned VLMs structurally lack — without pixel-level generative cost.

1. Motivation

  1. Pixel objectives overpay for detail. JEPA-family results (I-JEPA, V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2) show latent-space prediction yields better representations at a fraction of the compute — but with no language interface.
  2. Text-aligned encoders are blind to what SSL encoders see. CLIP/CoCa features carry little dense spatial structure (DINOv2's strength: parts, layout, geometry) or motion/physical dynamics (V-JEPA's strength). A prediction objective in those spaces injects exactly the missing information — while the input tower keeps OCR/VQA/grounding intact.
  3. Frozen targets remove JEPA's hardest engineering problem. No EMA targets or stop-gradients: frozen, precomputed targets make training a stationary-target problem — collapse is impossible, and the loop stays as simple as standard VLM training.

3. Method

3.1 Architecture: decoupled input and target spaces

  • Input tower: pretrained CLIP/CoCa encoder (existing heavily-trained checkpoint; optionally fine-tuned), projected into the LLM — preserves OCR, documents, VQA, grounding.
  • Target spaces (frozen, precomputed offline): image spans → DINOv2 (dense semantics, parts, geometry; precedent: DINO-WM, RCG) · video spans → V-JEPA 2 (motion, physics; precedent: V-JEPA 2-AC).
  • Separate lightweight heads per target space (shared trunk), each with its own per-dimension whitening — never mix target spaces in one head.

Design principle — complementarity: predicting SigLIP targets from a CLIP input is nearly redundant; predicting DINO/JEPA targets injects what the input space lacks.

3.2 What to predict: future, not same-frame

Targets are the latents of upcoming visual content — the next image in the interleaved sequence, or frames \(t+k\) given frames \(\le t\). Same-frame targets degenerate into CLIP→DINO feature translation (a distillation signal, kept only as a small auxiliary term). The primary loss is anticipatory: given what you see and read, predict what comes next.

3.3 Prediction objectives (ablation axis)

(a) Dense regression

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}} = \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i \|\hat z_i - z_i\|_2^2 \]

Simplest; dense gradient (MetaMorph: improves understanding); collapses multimodal futures to their mean. Optional VICReg-style variance regularizer on predictions.

(b) Contrastive (InfoNCE)

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{nce}} = -\log \frac{\exp(\text{sim}(\hat z_i, z_i)/\tau)}{\sum_j \exp(\text{sim}(\hat z_i, z_j)/\tau)} \]

Rank the true future above in-batch negatives — no mean-collapse, negligible cost, most JEPA-native (energy-based) fix.

(c) Discretized-latent cross-entropy

Quantize the frozen target space once (k-means or FSQ); predict codes with softmax CE. A categorical distribution natively represents multimodal futures — AR-over-tokens without VQ-on-pixels lossiness. Most scaling-friendly (clean per-token loss).

(d) Latent flow-matching head (MAR-style)

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}} = \mathbb{E}_{t,\epsilon}\| v_\theta(z_i^t, t, c_i) - (z_i - \epsilon) \|_2^2 \]

LLM emits conditioning \(c_i\); small MLP models the full continuous conditional. Sampleable rollouts; iterative inference (cheap: low-dim); needs schedules + whitening. The upper-bound treatment.

3.4 Optional: latent-to-pixel decoder

A diffusion decoder trained separately to invert the target encoders (RCG-style), for visualization/generation only; out of the main loop by design.

4. Hypotheses & experiments

H1 — Understanding

Latent prediction on frozen SSL targets improves an otherwise-identical baseline: spatial suites for DINO targets; temporal/physical suites (Perception Test, TempCompass) for JEPA targets. Because the input tower is CLIP/CoCa, OCR/DocVQA should be unchanged — a regression test, not a trade-off.

H2 — Target space matters (headline ablation)

Swap targets among SigLIP / DINOv2 / V-JEPA, all else fixed. Prediction: SigLIP ≈ no gain (redundant with input); DINOv2 wins spatial; V-JEPA wins temporal/embodied. The per-benchmark-family map is publishable on its own.

H3 — Objective

(b)/(c)/(d) ≥ (a) wherever futures are genuinely multimodal (video continuation, next-image-in-document); (a) stays competitive for understanding. Latent rollout metrics (feature PSNR, retrieval@k), planning success à la V-JEPA 2-AC / DINO-WM.

H4 — Efficiency

At matched FLOPs, better spatial+temporal scaling than a Transfusion-style pixel-diffusion baseline (0.3B/1B/3B ladder; objective (c) gives the cleanest scaling ordinate).

De-risking probe (week 1): linear-probe frozen DINOv2 + V-JEPA 2 features on all target evals before Stage-2 training; weak probes ⇒ revise target spaces first.

Minimal viable experiment: existing CLIP/CoCa tower + open 7B LLM + frozen DINOv2 targets with regression head, interleaved image-text data, targets precomputed. Baseline: same run, prediction loss off. Video/JEPA arm second.

6. What this is not

  • Not an image generator. Native synthesis is deliberately traded away; §3.4 recovers it approximately if needed.
  • Not joint JEPA training. No EMA/stop-grad anywhere; stability bought with frozen targets, at the cost of non-adaptable target spaces (probed in week 1, mapped in H2).
