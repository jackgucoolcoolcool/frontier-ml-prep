LatentFusion: Multimodal Language Models that Predict in Frozen Self-Supervised Latent Spaces

Research Proposal · July 2026 · draft v0.6
see in CLIP space predict in DINO/JEPA space frozen targets — no collapse no pixel-generative cost

Abstract

Current unified multimodal models couple language modeling with pixel-level generative objectives — discrete VQ tokens (Chameleon) or diffusion over image latents (Transfusion). Both spend capacity on high-frequency detail irrelevant for reasoning, and neither exploits the strong predictive representations learned by self-supervised vision models. LatentFusion is a language model that consumes images through a standard text-aligned vision tower (CLIP/CoCa) but is trained to predict upcoming visual content in frozen self-supervised latent spaces — DINOv2 for image spans (dense semantics, geometry) and video-JEPA for video spans (dynamics, physics). Input and target spaces are deliberately decoupled: the model sees in a text-aligned space and predicts in spaces encoding exactly what text alignment lacks. Targets are frozen and precomputed, so collapse is impossible by construction. We study a spectrum of objectives — dense regression, InfoNCE, discretized-latent cross-entropy (including an extended-vocabulary variant that is literally standard LLM training), and a latent flow head — as progressively stronger treatments of regression-to-the-mean. Hypothesis: stronger spatial, temporal, and world-model capability per FLOP than pixel-generative training, trading away native synthesis (recoverable via a separately trained decoder).

One-line pitch: a language model that sees in CLIP space and learns to predict in DINO/JEPA space acquires the dense-spatial and temporal world knowledge that text-aligned VLMs structurally lack — without pixel-level generative cost.
Figure 1. The main idea. A causal LM reads interleaved text and CLIP-encoded visuals; at every visual span it is trained to predict the frozen DINOv2/V-JEPA latents of the upcoming visual content (Δ>0). Text keeps ordinary cross-entropy; no pixels are generated; targets are precomputed and frozen, so representation collapse is impossible.

1. Motivation

  1. Pixel objectives overpay for detail. JEPA-family results (I-JEPA, V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2) show latent-space prediction yields better representations at a fraction of the compute — but with no language interface.
  2. Text-aligned encoders are blind to what SSL encoders see. CLIP/CoCa features carry little dense spatial structure (DINOv2: parts, layout, geometry) or motion/physics (V-JEPA). Predicting in those spaces injects exactly the missing information — while the input tower keeps OCR/VQA/grounding intact.
  3. Frozen targets remove JEPA's hardest engineering problem. No EMA targets or stop-gradients: precomputed frozen targets make training a stationary-target problem — collapse is impossible.
The input space supplies what the model reads; the target spaces supply what it must learn to anticipate.

2. Related work (positioning)

Three converging threads: (i) feature prediction as a VLM auxiliary objective (MetaMorph, ROSS, Emu/Emu2; REPA in the mirror direction); (ii) world models in frozen SSL spaces — without language (DINO-WM, V-JEPA 2-AC, IWM); (iii) semantic visual tokenizers (SEED, LaViT, VILA-U; Janus's decoupled encoders).

2.1 Design-space analysis: five axes

Every method above is a point in a five-dimensional design space. Making the axes explicit shows where prior work clusters — and why our coordinates are chosen, not arbitrary.

The unexplored cell: (A1 = text-aligned, A2 = dense/video-SSL, A3 = Δ>0, A4 = distribution-aware, A5 = frozen).

What each neighbor proves — and leaves open

  • REPA ⇒ A2: DINO targets carry dense signal even in a generative host.
  • ROSS/MetaMorph ⇒ A3 floor: even Δ=0 helps understanding; our Δ>0 claim builds on top — and setting Δ=0 reproduces them as a built-in ablation.
  • DINO-WM / V-JEPA 2-AC ⇒ A2×A3: frozen SSL spaces are predictable enough to plan in — minus language.
  • MAR/RCG ⇒ A4: low-dim latent distributional heads work; Emu2's blur shows point-estimates failing on multimodal futures.

Axis interactions (why coordinates cohere)

  • A1↔A2 (Janus-style decoupling): complementarity only exists because input ≠ target; SigLIP targets from a CLIP input ≈ no new information.
  • A3↔A4: distributional objectives only earn their cost when Δ>0 — at Δ=0 the target is deterministic and L2 suffices.
  • A5↔everything: frozen targets make A2 swappable offline (H2 is cheap), A4 stable (stationary loss), and training infra identical to a standard VLM. Cost: non-adaptable space — exactly what gates D0–D1 measure first.

2.2 What does each visual objective actually learn?

Five training paradigms dominate visual representation learning. They differ not in how much they learn but in what decides which information is kept — and that selector is the deepest reason the target-space choice (A2) matters.

The selector is the objective's identity

(1)/(2) are text-bounded — they cannot learn what language doesn't say. (3) is augmentation-bounded — invariances are hand-chosen. (4) is predictability-bounded — it keeps exactly the world-model-relevant bits and drops noise because it is unpredictable. (5) is unbounded — and therefore unfocused: capacity is allocated by pixel entropy, not by usefulness.

This predicts H2's outcome pattern

Gains should appear exactly on benchmarks probing the selector gap between input and target space — spatial for DINO targets, temporal for JEPA targets — and nowhere else. Empirical anchors: MMVP failures are (1)-signatures; DINOv2 dense-probe dominance is a (3)-signature; V-JEPA 2-AC planning is a (4)-signature; REPA shows (5) does not subsume (3) even with more compute. If gains appear off-pattern, the complementarity theory is wrong in an interesting way.

3. Method

3.1 Architecture: decoupled input and target spaces

Input and target streams never mix; target encoders never run in the training loop.

Design principle — complementarity: predicting SigLIP targets from a CLIP input is nearly redundant; predicting DINO/JEPA targets injects what the input space lacks. Separate heads per target space (different dims/statistics — never mix them in one head).

3.2 What to predict: future, not same-frame

Main design risk, handled up front: predicting the latents of the same frame the tower is encoding degenerates into CLIP→DINO feature translation — distillation, not world modeling. Targets are the latents of upcoming content: the next image in the interleaved sequence, or frames \(t+k\) given frames \(\le t\). Same-frame translation is kept only as a small auxiliary term, with a translation-only control run for comparison.

3.3 The loss, concretely, in a standard causal LM

Everything stays next-token prediction under the ordinary causal mask; only what the head outputs changes at positions whose next slot is visual:

\[ \mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\sum_{i:\,x_{i+1}\in\text{text}} -\log p_W(x_{i+1}\mid h_i)}_{\text{unchanged text CE}} \;+\; \lambda \underbrace{\sum_{i:\,x_{i+1}\in\text{visual}} \ell\big(g(h_i),\, \bar z_{i+1}\big)}_{\text{latent prediction}}, \qquad \bar z = \text{whiten}(z) \]
Blue = unchanged text CE. Teal = latent loss ℓ ∈ {L2, InfoNCE, discrete-CE, flow}. v-slots are CLIP-tower embeddings; z̄ are whitened frozen targets.
# drop-in on any HF-style causal LM — causal mask untouched
h = model.transformer(inputs_embeds=emb).last_hidden_state    # [B, L, D]

# text loss — unchanged
L_text = F.cross_entropy(lm_head(h[:, :-1]).transpose(1, 2), tokens[:, 1:],
                         reduction='none')[text_next_mask[:, 1:]].mean()

# latent loss — positions whose NEXT slot is a visual target
pred  = dino_head(h[:, :-1][visual_next_mask[:, 1:]])          # [M, d_dino]
tgt   = (z_dino - mu) / sigma                                  # precomputed, whitened, frozen
L_reg = F.mse_loss(pred, tgt)                                  # or InfoNCE / discrete-CE / flow

loss = L_text + lam * L_reg

Input embeddings for visual slots come from the CLIP/CoCa tower through a projector; targets z ship from the dataloader like labels. For future prediction the target at an image span is the next span's latents — same code, different alignment.

3.4 Prediction objectives (ablation axis)

(a) Dense regression

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}} = \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i \|\hat z_i - z_i\|_2^2 \]

Simplest; dense gradient (MetaMorph: improves understanding); collapses multimodal futures to their mean. Optional VICReg-style variance regularizer.

(b) Contrastive (InfoNCE)

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{nce}} = -\log \tfrac{\exp(\mathrm{sim}(\hat z_i, z_i)/\tau)}{\sum_j \exp(\mathrm{sim}(\hat z_i, z_j)/\tau)} \]

Rank the true future above in-batch negatives — no mean-collapse, negligible cost, most JEPA-native (energy-based) fix.

(c / c′) Discretized-latent cross-entropy

Quantize the frozen target space once (k-means / FSQ); predict codes with softmax CE — a categorical natively represents multimodal futures. (c′): add the codes to the LLM vocabulary and predict through the normal LM head — the whole system is literally standard LLM training: one loss, perplexity + scaling curves for free. Caveat: codes must quantize semantic spaces (DINO/JEPA, SEED/LaViT-style) — pixel-VQ codes would smuggle texture back in and revert to Chameleon.

(d) Latent flow-matching head (MAR-style)

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}} = \mathbb{E}_{t,\epsilon}\|v_\theta(z_i^t, t, c_i) - (z_i - \epsilon)\|_2^2 \]

LLM emits conditioning \(c_i\); small MLP models the full continuous conditional. Sampleable rollouts; iterative inference (cheap: low-dim); needs schedules + whitening. Upper-bound treatment.

3.5 Text-side prediction: narrated rollouts (captioning arm)

Prediction can also run in the reverse direction: from predicted future latents back into language. Add a caption objective conditioned on the predicted latent \(\hat z\) (not the ground-truth image):

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{cap}} = -\sum_t \log p\big(w_t \mid w_{with \(w\) a caption of the future frame (video ASR/alt-text, or synthetic). This is the CoCa lesson — contrastive and captioning teach complementary things — imported into the predictive setting. It buys three things:

Grounding pressure

\(\hat z\) must stay decodable into language, blocking degenerate solutions that satisfy the latent loss in regions of the frozen space carrying no usable semantics.

A narrated world model

Latent rollout → caption yields human-readable imagined futures (“the ball will bounce off the table”) — free interpretability for H3, and a natural VLA interface: describe the predicted consequence of an action before executing it.

A text-metric for latent quality

Caption quality from \(\hat z\) vs. from the true \(z\) directly measures how much semantics prediction preserves — cheaper than training probe heads.

Risks

Future-caption data is noisier (ASR misalignment); the model may shortcut — inferring the caption from surrounding text context rather than \(\hat z\). Mitigation: gradient-stop text context into the caption head; context-ablated caption eval.

3.6 Optional: latent-to-pixel decoder

A diffusion decoder trained separately to invert the target encoders (RCG-style), for visualization/generation only; out of the main loop by design.

4. Hypotheses & experiments

H1 — Understanding

Latent prediction on frozen SSL targets improves an otherwise-identical baseline: spatial suites for DINO targets; temporal/physical suites (Perception Test, TempCompass) for JEPA targets. Input tower is CLIP/CoCa, so OCR/DocVQA should be unchanged — a regression test, not a trade-off.

H2 — Target space matters (headline)

Swap targets among SigLIP / DINOv2 / V-JEPA, all else fixed. Prediction: SigLIP ≈ no gain (redundant with input); DINOv2 wins spatial; V-JEPA wins temporal/embodied. The per-benchmark-family map is publishable on its own.

H3 — Objective

(b)/(c)/(d) ≥ (a) wherever futures are genuinely multimodal (video continuation, next-image-in-document); (a) stays competitive for understanding. Latent rollout metrics (feature PSNR, retrieval@k), planning success à la V-JEPA 2-AC / DINO-WM.

H4 — Efficiency

At matched FLOPs, better spatial+temporal scaling than a Transfusion-style pixel-diffusion baseline (0.3B/1B/3B ladder; objective (c′) gives the cleanest scaling ordinate).

6. What this is not

  • Not an image generator. Native synthesis is deliberately traded away; §3.6 recovers it approximately if needed.
  • Not joint JEPA training. No EMA/stop-grad anywhere; stability bought with frozen targets, at the cost of non-adaptable target spaces (probed in week 1, mapped in H2).

7. Timeline

8. Extension M — Motion as a target space, predicted by a diffusion head

Added in v0.6. Motion (point tracks / flow) is the middle rung between pixels (too much) and symbolic laws (too hard): it is where the physics lives with appearance factored out. This section adds target space M to the A2 axis and works the design all the way down to code.

8.1 Why motion targets

Physics lives in motion

A falling ball's texture is irrelevant; its trajectory is the physics. Predicting motion predicts the law's consequences without deriving symbols — sidestepping the grounding problem of "extract F=ma from latents."

Frozen targets, same recipe

Off-the-shelf trackers (CoTracker, TAPIR) and flow models (RAFT) give pseudo-labels at scale. A frozen tracker plays exactly the role of the frozen DINO/JEPA encoder: precomputed, stationary, no collapse, ships like labels (A5 unchanged).

Low-dim, multimodal, plannable

K×T×2 track tensors are ~10³ dims; futures are genuinely multimodal (ball bounces left or right); and diffusion inpainting turns the predictor into a goal-conditioned planner for free.

Verifiable, closed loop

Predicted tracks → motion-prompted video generation → re-track → consistency check. And fitting a parabola to a predicted trajectory is trivial — physics-consistency becomes measurable without symbolic derivation.

Prior-work slots: Diffusion Policy (diffusion over action trajectories beats regression because of multimodality), MotionDiffuser (multi-agent trajectory diffusion), MDM/PhysDiff (text-conditioned motion diffusion + physics projection in the sampling loop), DDVM (diffusion on flow fields), Diffuser (inpainting-as-planning), ATM/Track2Act (tracks as the interface for manipulation), Motion Prompting (tracks as input conditioning for video gen). Every ingredient is individually de-risked; the open slot is the language-conditioned predictive direction: interleaved text+video in → future motion out.

8.3 Spending a pretrained video model (Veo/omni/Transfusion-class)

Decision rule: denoise in the smallest space that contains the answer; condition on the richest space you can get frozen. The answer (future motion) lives in ~10³ dims — diffuse there. The knowledge (visual dynamics prior) lives in the base model — pipe it in three ways, never as the diffusion arena:
  1. Features as conditioning. For a Transfusion-style unified model there is no separate encoder — the backbone transformer is the encoder, and its middle-layer hidden states at visual positions are the feature bank (DIFT: mid-network diffusion features encode correspondence; Marigold: generative priors transfer through light adaptation). Sweep layer index and noise level t with a linear probe for next-step velocity — that sweep is the D0 gate here. Retrospective features (context positions, one forward pass) are the default; prospective features (append noised future span, partial denoise, read future positions — features of the model's imagination) are the compute-vs-prior ablation.
  2. Rollouts as teacher. Sample base-model continuations, re-track them, use resulting tracks as extra pseudo-labels — the base model's physics prior distills into the cheap head, paid at training time only. Bonus: student-on-real vs. student-on-rollouts directly measures how physical the base model's imagination is.
  3. Renderer for verification. Predicted tracks → motion-prompted generation → re-track → agreement.

Diffusing in the video model's own space (fine-tune to joint video+motion denoising) is right only when the deliverable is controllable generation or when base-model iteration cost is not yours — a product path, not this research program.

8.4 The motion head, concretely

Setup: K query points q on the last context frame; target τ ∈ ℝK×T×2 = future per-step deltas (CoTracker pseudo-labels, visibility-masked).

8.4.1 The load-bearing subtlety: how does a query point q map into H?

The whole head hinges on getting a backbone feature "at" each point. Whether that is even well-defined depends on how the backbone tokenizes — two regimes, then the fix that survives both:

(i) Per-frame latent patches — map is a computable affine (but soft)

Single-image Transfusion-style tokenization (VAE latents → patchify → grid): q_pixel → /vae_downsample → /patch_size → (row,col) → token. The correspondence exists. Two things make it soft, not wrong: receptive-field spread (VAE latents integrate a pixel neighborhood — sub-patch motion lives below the grid) and contextual mixing (after attention, H[:,i] keeps patch i's position but its content is globally contaminated). Bilinear-sampling the position is fine here; treating the value as purely local is not.

(ii) Spatiotemporal tubes / perceiver tokens — map does not exist

A 3D-VAE token is a spacetime volume (e.g. 4 frames × 8×8 px → 1 token), so "q at frame t" has no 2D slice to land on. Any real video backbone is likely here — which is exactly why naive bilinear_sample(H, q) is hand-wavy.

Robust fix — stop indexing, start attending. Make q a query token carrying a space-time positional embedding; let it cross-attend over all of H, whose keys are tagged with their own space-time coordinates. This uses position and content, degrades gracefully when position alone is ambiguous, is tokenizer-agnostic, and is exactly what real point trackers (CoTracker/TAPIR) do — they correlate a query feature against a cost volume, never index. Reuse the backbone's own position scheme (e.g. RoPE) so query and keys share one coordinate system.

Cleaner still — decouple the two roles. f_local was serving two masters: local precision ("exactly which pixel") and global prior ("what's the scene physics"). H is good at the second, unreliable at the first. So take local precision from a pixel-aligned encoder with a guaranteed clean map (a small CNN, or the frozen tracker's own cost volume — already computing correspondence) and use H only via cross-attention for the global prior. The q→H indexing problem then disappears from the precision-critical path: the tracker knows where, the backbone knows what happens next. M-D0 is the arbiter — linear-probe H at q's nominal location for q's own current velocity; if even that fails, the naive map is dead in your backbone → go straight to attention readout / decoupled encoder.

# ---- q → H readout: cross-attention lookup, NOT naive indexing (see 8.4.1) ----
q_tok   = mlp_q(cat(fourier(q), time_embed(t_query)))     # [K,512] "a point here, at this time"
H_kv    = h_proj + posemb(H_positions)                    # [N,512] keys tagged w/ space-time coords (reuse RoPE)
f_local = cross_attention(q_tok, key=H_kv, val=H_kv)      # [K,512] soft learned lookup (pos + content)
#         ↑ for a pixel-aligned encoder E, use f_local = bilinear_sample(E, q) and keep H for global cross-attn only

# ---- K track tokens: each knows where it is, what the backbone believes there,
#      and what its noisy trajectory currently looks like ----
e_pos   = fourier(q)                     # [K, 64] position (Fourier: beats raw xy — spectral bias)
e_traj  = mlp_in(tau_noisy.flatten(1))   # [K,256] current noisy trajectory (T*2 -> 256)
e_t     = timestep_embed(t)              # [256]   flow-matching time, broadcast-added (DiT-style)
tokens  = mlp_fuse(cat(f_local, e_pos, e_traj)) + e_t      # [K, 512]

# ---- denoiser: self-attn couples points (rigidity); cross-attn reads scene + text ----
z      = dit_blocks(tokens, context=h_proj)                # h_proj = linear(h): [N, 512]
v_pred = mlp_out(z).reshape(K, T, 2)     # velocity field, NOT the trajectory itself

# ---- training: flow matching — target velocity along a straight path is constant ----
tau0, t = randn_like(tau1), rand()
tau_t   = (1-t)*tau0 + t*tau1            # noise→data interpolation point
loss    = (vis_mask * (head(tau_t,t,q,h) - (tau1-tau0))**2).mean()   # occlusions masked

# ---- sampling: 10 Euler steps, CFG w≈2; rerun with fresh noise = another future ----
tau = randn(K, T, 2)
for t in linspace(0, 1, 10):
    v_c, v_u = head(tau,t,q,h), head(tau,t,q,null)         # conditioned / unconditional
    tau += dt * (v_u + w*(v_c - v_u))                      # amplify what conditioning changes
tracks = q[:,None,:] + cumsum(tau, dim=1)                  # deltas -> absolute positions

The two load-bearing details

The q→H readout (§8.4.1) is where correctness lives — cross-attention lookup keyed by space-time position, or a decoupled pixel-aligned encoder, not naive indexing into a possibly-tubed token grid. Temporal span of h: motion is invisible in one frame — feed hiddens from ≥2 context frames, or difference consecutive feature maps as an explicit "neural flow" channel.

Why flow matching represents multimodality

At low t the model cannot tell which future this sample is, so it predicts the average velocity; as t grows, τ_t leaks which mode it is in and the model commits. One deterministic network, full multimodal distribution. Inpainting = one line: after each Euler step, overwrite clamped entries (goal endpoint, known past) renoised to level t — the rest bends to fit ⇒ goal-conditioned planning free.

Gates & baselines (extends the D-ladder)

M-D0: layer/timestep linear-probe sweep for next-step velocity; three-way conditioning comparison (backbone hiddens vs. V-JEPA vs. DINO) — publishable alone. M-D1: copy-last-velocity baseline; any head must clear it widely. M-D2: same head, diffusion machinery deleted (direct L2 regression) — flow matching must beat it on min-ADE wherever futures are multimodal, else the machinery isn't earning its keep.

Risks

Tracker pseudo-labels noisy under occlusion/fast motion (confidence-weighted loss, visibility flags); camera motion contaminates object motion (stabilize, or predict camera-compensated coordinates); backbone hiddens optimized to render may over-represent appearance (that's what the M-D0 sweep measures); K×T token budget in interleaved sequences.

Eval: min-over-N ADE/FDE (honest under multimodality), sample diversity, rigid-body coherence of samples, CFG-sweep of language influence on physics, and the closed loop: sampled tracks → motion-prompted generation → re-track → agreement. Physics payoff without symbols: fit parabolas/conservation to predicted tracks — "does error grow exactly where physics is violated?" becomes measurable, no symbolic derivation required.
