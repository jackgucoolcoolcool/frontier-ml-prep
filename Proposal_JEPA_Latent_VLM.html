LatentFusion: Multimodal Language Models that Predict in a Frozen Video-JEPA Space

Research Proposal · Yiming Gu · July 2026 · draft v0.1

Abstract

Current unified multimodal models couple language modeling with pixel-level generative objectives — discrete VQ tokens (Chameleon) or diffusion over image latents (Transfusion). Both spend capacity on high-frequency visual detail irrelevant for reasoning, and neither exploits the strong predictive representations learned by self-supervised video models. We propose LatentFusion: (1) pretrain a JEPA-style encoder on large-scale video and freeze it; (2) train a language model on interleaved image/video–text data where visual spans are represented — and predicted — as frozen JEPA latents, via dense regression, a lightweight latent diffusion/flow head (MAR-style), or both. We hypothesize this yields stronger temporal, physical, and world-model capability per FLOP than pixel-generative training, trading away native image synthesis (recoverable via a separately trained decoder). The frozen-target design eliminates representation collapse by construction.

One-line pitch: a language model trained to autoregressively predict the future in a frozen video-JEPA space learns a textual world model — temporal and physical understanding that pixel-generative and contrastive-encoder VLMs structurally lack.

1. Motivation

  1. Pixel objectives overpay for detail. JEPA-family results (I-JEPA, V-JEPA, V-JEPA 2) show latent-space prediction yields better representations at a fraction of the compute — but with no language interface.
  2. Video-SSL features encode dynamics that text-aligned encoders miss. CLIP/SigLIP encoders are trained on static image–text alignment; they carry little motion or physical intuition. V-JEPA 2 latents support zero-shot planning (V-JEPA 2-AC). No current VLM predicts in such a space.
  3. Freezing removes JEPA's hardest engineering problem. Joint latent-prediction training needs EMA targets and stop-gradients to avoid collapse. With a frozen Stage-1 encoder, Stage 2 is a stationary-target regression problem: collapse is impossible and training stays as simple as standard VLM training.

3. Method

Stage 1 — Frozen video-JEPA encoder

Pretrain (or reuse: V-JEPA 2 ViT-g) a spatiotemporal encoder \(f\) with the standard JEPA objective. After pretraining, online and target encoders coincide; freeze \(f\) permanently and precompute latents \(z = f(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\) offline — Stage 2 never runs the vision tower.

Stage 2 — Interleaved LLM training

Text positions get next-token cross-entropy. Visual spans are predicted from prior context via:

(a) Dense regression head

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}} = \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i \big\| \hat z_i - z_i \big\|_2^2 \quad (\text{or } 1-\cos(\hat z_i, z_i)) \]

Simplest; strong dense gradient into the LLM (MetaMorph: improves understanding); regression-to-the-mean under genuine uncertainty.

(b) Latent diffusion / flow-matching head (MAR-style)

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}} = \mathbb{E}_{t,\epsilon}\big\| v_\theta(z_i^t, t, c_i) - (z_i - \epsilon) \big\|_2^2 \]

LLM emits conditioning vector \(c_i\); small MLP models the full conditional \(p(z\mid\text{context})\). Needs per-dim latent normalization (cf. Emu2, RCG); adds sampling cost.

(c) Both

\[ \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}} + \lambda_r\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}} + \lambda_f\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{flow}} \]

Regression as dense auxiliary signal + flow head for distributional prediction. The (a)/(b)/(c) comparison is a headline experiment.

Optional Stage 3 — Latent-to-pixel decoder

A diffusion decoder trained separately to invert \(f\) (RCG-style), for visualization/generation only; kept out of the main loop by design.

4. Hypotheses & experiments

H1 — Understanding

Latent prediction on frozen JEPA targets improves temporal/physical benchmarks over an identical no-prediction-loss baseline (MetaMorph-style ablation). Perception Test, TempCompass, physical reasoning.

H2 — Target space matters (main scientific risk)

Video-JEPA targets beat SigLIP targets on temporal/embodied tasks and lose on OCR/documents. Mitigation arm: dual input streams (SigLIP + JEPA in, JEPA-only targets).

H3 — Distributional head

Flow ≥ regression for rollout/planning quality; regression ≥ flow for understanding; combined is Pareto-best. Latent rollout metrics (feature PSNR, retrieval@k of true future), planning success à la V-JEPA 2-AC.

H4 — Efficiency

At matched FLOPs, better temporal-understanding scaling than a Transfusion-style pixel-diffusion baseline (0.3B/1B/3B ladder; loss-vs-compute and benchmark-vs-compute curves).

De-risking probe (week 1): linear-probe frozen V-JEPA 2 features on all target evals before Stage 2; weak probes ⇒ revise the target space first.

Minimal viable experiment: frozen V-JEPA 2 ViT-L + open 7B LLM + regression head on an interleaved video-text mix; latents precomputed; baseline = same data, prediction loss off.

6. What this is not

  • Not an image generator. Native synthesis is deliberately traded away; Stage 3 recovers it approximately if needed.
  • Not joint JEPA training. No EMA/stop-grad in Stage 2; stability is bought with the frozen encoder, at the cost of a non-adaptable target space (tested in H2).
